##HOOK
You set a screw against a stud, press the button, and the little screwdriver just hums and stops. It isn't your wrist. It isn't the screw. Bob Vila tested a batch of four-volt screwdrivers, and several could not drive screws into hardwood. Reviewed hit the same wall, with a model that could not sink a screw into a two-by-four without a pilot hole. Then the boxes make it worse. One says five newton-meters. Another says forty-four inch-pounds. Those are the same number. So here is the rule for this whole video. Four volts is for furniture and cover plates. Twelve volts is for studs. I read Bob Vila, Reviewed, and Pro Tool Reviews, and checked every listing. Six made the list. I will also warn you off a top pick you can no longer buy new. And number one drives the stud the small ones could not.

##P6
Number six, the SKIL Twist 2.0 SD5619-01 4V Cordless Screwdriver with Pivoting Head.

This is for the renter or first-time owner who needs a screwdriver for flat-pack furniture, cabinet knobs, and switch plates.

It runs on a four-volt battery built into the handle, and it charges through a USB-C port. Pro Tool Reviews lists it at two hundred and twenty RPM and twenty-two inch-pounds of torque. That is low on purpose, so it is hard to strip a small screw. Three torque settings let you dial it down even further. The best trick is the head. It pivots and locks at zero, twenty-two and a half, and forty-five degrees. That gives you a better view of the screw, and it helps you reach into tight corners. There is an LED light, a one-finger forward and reverse button, and a battery level gauge. It weighs about one pound. In the box you get the screwdriver, a USB-C cable, and two two-inch bits, one Phillips and one slotted.

Pro Tool Reviews picked it as their best electric screwdriver for the money.

Now, the honest limits. There is no speed control. And remember the hook: at twenty-two inch-pounds, this is not a stud driver. Only two bits come in the box, so plan on a bit set.

Its place in the lineup is the budget pick.

Buy it if you assemble furniture. Do not buy it for decks or framing.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any screwdriver on this list.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not a lab test.

First, match the bit to the screw. A number two Phillips fits most household screws, and a worn bit slips out and chews the head.

Second, start on the lowest clutch or torque setting, and step up until the screw sits flush.

Third, drill a pilot hole in hardwood and near the end of a board, so the wood does not split.

Fourth, push straight in line with the screw, and let the tool do the turning.

Fifth, on inline models, finish the last turn by hand.

Sixth, store lithium tools with some charge, not dead flat, and out of a hot car.

##TAIL
Before you buy, ask one question: what will this screw go into? Furniture, cover plates, and cabinet knobs are four-volt jobs. Studs, decks, and hardwood are twelve-volt jobs. And when a box says newton-meters, multiply by about nine to get inch-pounds.

All seven links are in the description, including the DEWALT gyroscopic. Check the voltage, the torque in one unit, the clutch settings, the grip style, and whether the battery is built in or swappable.

Here is the quick recap. The SKIL Twist is the budget pick. The BLACK+DECKER is the flashlight pick. The DEWALT FlexDrive is the inline pick. The Makita is the convertible pick. The DEWALT twelve-volt is the compact pick. And for most people, the Bosch PS21-2A.
