##HOOK
Every fuel transfer pump is sold by one number, gallons per minute. That number is the lie this category runs on. Alberta Agriculture's farm machinery lab put one of the most common twelve volt pumps on a diesel tank, with a fully charged battery. It measured eight percent under its rating. Then they added three feet of suction and nine feet of rise, and it lost another nine percent. The maker's own manual admits it. Its flow curves assume three feet of lift, and actual flow may vary. So you will hear which number to trust, and why the box number is not the flow you get at the hose. You will hear about the pump reviewers call the best value that is a straw on a farm tank. And there is a free check that gets your lost flow back. All the product links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works on any pump.

Most lost flow is not the motor. It is restriction. Fill-Rite's manual says to expect a loss of flow whenever the nozzle sits higher than the pump. Fill-Rite also says a ten micron filter restricts more than a thirty micron one. And it says to change the filter every six months, or as soon as the flow drops. So keep the hose short, the nozzle low, and the inlet screen clean. Then time a five gallon fill when the pump is new, and write the seconds on the tank. Every spring, time it again. If it got slower, the filter or the screen is talking to you.

That check costs nothing, and it decides whether you replace a pump or keep one.
