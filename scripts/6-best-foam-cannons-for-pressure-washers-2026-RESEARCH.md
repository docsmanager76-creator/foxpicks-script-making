## 3. Buyer checklist
1. Your pressure washer's PSI and GPM vs the cannon's stated minimum/maximum.
2. Orifice size: lower-pressure washers need a smaller orifice for thick foam (MJJC ships 1.1/1.25/1.35 mm; DUSICHIN-type 1.04 mm).
3. Fitting: 1/4" quick connect, and whether your gun or wand uses it.
4. Bottle size and mouth width; plastic vs metal fittings; leak resistance.
5. Soap-to-water dial and spray fan adjustment.
6. Weight and balance when full.
7. Warranty term.
8. Care: rinse after use, avoid wax-based soaps (clogs the filter).

## 5. Hook — type 3 Reader's Pain (not 6)
You hook up a foam cannon and get watery soap, not shaving-cream foam. Reason is usually a mismatch between the cannon, your washer's pressure, and flow, not the soap. Every box in this video quotes a PSI range, and the ranges differ (800 to 4,000). Open loops: villain, free upgrade.

## 6. Myth
"A foam cannon works the same on any pressure washer." The minimums run from 800 PSI to 1,000 PSI and 1.3 to 2.0 GPM; Car and Driver tuned every cannon to the same mix to compare.

## 7. Villain — type D Functional Failure: Armor All 2-in-1 Foam Cannon Kit
Car and Driver (March 2026): foam did not aerate well, dispensed too heavily, splotches and clumps; the hose side is weaker. Listing: 40 oz reservoir, 500-3,500 PSI, garden hose adapter. Its strength is versatility, not foam. Not a pick (never), after #4, redirect to #3 and #2. No prices spoken.

## 9. Free upgrade (≈128-135 words)
Look up your washer's PSI and GPM on its label; compare to the cannon's minimum; for lower pressure use the smaller orifice; fill with warm water and the soap first; turn the foam knob to the thickest setting then back off; rinse with clean water for thirty seconds after use; avoid wax-based soaps; check the 1/4" fitting before ordering. No purchase needed.
