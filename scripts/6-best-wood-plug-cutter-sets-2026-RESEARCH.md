## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST

Built from Fine Woodworking (Pekovich 2017 review + 2025 and 2005 forum threads), the makers' own instructions (Snappy, Montana, DKIBBITH, aioejir, Milescraft), and Veritas and Kreg product copy. **No outlet publishes a scored test rubric for this category.**

1. **Plug style**: tapered (wedges into the hole, gap-free; FWW's preference for screw holes), straight (also cuts tenons and dowels), or both in one set
2. **Labeled size vs actual plug size**: does "1/2 in" mean a plug for a 1/2 in hole? (Milescraft's own instructions shift one size; metric-relabeled sets: 6/10/13/16 mm sold as 1/4–5/8)
3. **Hand drill or drill press**: does it have a centering tip? Most cutters have no spur and wander by hand
4. **Shank**: 1/4 in hex quick-change, 1/2 in hex, 1/2 in round, or 10 mm round (chuck fit, slipping)
5. **Size range and count**: real sizes vs pieces (8 pieces = 4 sizes × 2 styles)
6. **Plug length / depth**: short screw-hole plugs vs deep plugs and tenons (Grizzly: up to 2-1/8 in; WEN: up to 3 in)
7. **Steel**: carbon, "65#", "45#", HSS, alloy (heat and burning; listings often contradict themselves)
8. **Flutes / edges**: 2 vs 4 flutes, multi-edge (Veritas: 4 flutes cut smoother)
9. **Burning and speed**: low RPM; "black ring" on plugs (FWW forum); aioejir listing warns it smokes if too fast
10. **Plug ejection / removal**: self-ejecting (Milescraft, through 3/4 in stock) vs bandsaw release
11. **Case and storage**: wooden box, plastic case, or none
12. **Made in / warranty**

## 5. HOOK — type **1 Collision** (last script used 3; allowed)

Three cutters all printed "1/2 inch", and none of them agree:
- one cuts a plug for a 1/2-inch hole (Snappy-type),
- one hardware-aisle set tells you in its own instructions to use the **bigger** cutter for the **middle** hole (Milescraft: "largest bit to fill hole made with middle bit"),
- and an imported set prints 1/2 inch on a **13 mm** cutter (Baorder listing: "1/4; 3/8; 1/2; 5/8 (6mm/10mm/13mm/16mm)").

Named outlet in the hook: **Fine Woodworking**. A member's cheap "half-inch" cutter cut plugs **under** half an inch (2025 forum), and Pekovich's 2017 hand-drill test. No pick brands in the hook.
Backup: **5 Single Absurd Number**: 6 mm sold as 1/4 inch is 0.236 vs 0.250, so the plug is loose before glue.

## 6. MYTH — "the size on the label is the plug you get"

- Milescraft listing: the plug from the largest bit fills the hole from the middle bit.
- Baorder listing: imperial sizes are metric (6/10/13/16 mm). 10 mm = 0.394 in vs 3/8 = 0.375, so too fat; 6 mm = 0.236 vs 1/4 = 0.250, so too loose.
- FWW forum 2025: a "half-inch" cutter cut undersize plugs; "12mm / 1/2 inch" are not the same.
- Veritas copy: quill runout makes holes oversize, so a tapered plug forgives it.
- Line: *"You are buying a plug size, not a cutter size."* Plant at #6/#5, pay at #2 (Snappy, which states its sizes as plug sizes; **verify Snappy's hole-to-plug wording at the script stage**).

## 9. FREE UPGRADE

The plug is half the job; the grain is the other half. Cut plugs from the same board (offcut) and mark grain direction on each plug, then line it up with the board when you glue (FWW forum 2005). Spin the cutter before it touches wood and run it slow; plunge and come back up fast so the plug doesn't get a black burn ring (FWW forum 2005 + DKIBBITH listing). Release plugs with masking tape on top and one bandsaw cut, not a screwdriver pry (FWW forum). Test-fit in scrap first, since labels lie (§ 6). **RPM numbers not spoken** (no maker chart found).
