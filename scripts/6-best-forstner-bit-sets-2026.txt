##HOOK
Bob Vila tested eight Forstner bit kits in oak, pine, and plywood, drilling single, overlapping, and angled holes. Here is what the box will never tell you. A fourteen piece set is not always fourteen sizes. Two sets on this list print the quarter inch bit twice, so fourteen pieces drill thirteen different holes. Piece count is the number every box shouts, and it is the weakest way to choose. What decides your holes is the cutting edge, the steel behind it, and whether your drill can turn it. You will hear why carbide is not automatically the better metal. You will meet a set that won an award, then bogged down both drills in the same test. And there is a free drilling habit that works with any bit you buy. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the BAIDETS BD210034 8-Piece Forstner Bit Set.

This is for someone who needs clean, flat bottomed holes for hinges, knobs, and dowels.

You get eight bits, and every one is a different size. They run from a quarter inch through three eighths, half, five eighths, three quarters, seven eighths, one inch, and one and three eighths. So eight pieces really means eight holes, with no duplicates. Hold that number, because a set several tiers above it gives you fourteen pieces and only thirteen sizes. Each bit is carbon steel, three and a half inches long, on a three eighths inch round shank that fits a regular drill chuck or a drill press. A center tooth locates the hole and keeps the bit from walking as it starts. BAIDETS says the toothed, beveled edge clears chips easily and keeps friction heat down. The listing names wood, plywood, and plastic.

Now, the honest limits. No major outlet has tested this set, so everything here comes from the maker's own listing. Carbon steel is softer than high speed steel, so it dulls faster if you run it hot. And the set stops at one and three eighths, with nothing for a two inch hole.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick, and the maker does not publish a warranty.

Buy it for hinges and hardware, and skip it if you need bigger holes.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The Hercules 8-Piece Carbide Tipped Forstner Bit Set.

A Forstner bit your drill cannot turn is a paperweight with extra steps.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it costs nothing but patience.

The enemy is heat. A bigger bit has a wider rim, so at the same drill speed its edge travels much faster through the wood. That edge heats up, the wood scorches, and the steel dulls. So the bigger the bit, the slower you run it. Speed charts disagree on exact numbers, but every one agrees on that direction. Fine Woodworking found the cleaner cutting bits also ran cooler. The eight kit testers warned that overheating shortens edge life, and Forstner bits cannot be sharpened on a bench grinder. So clamp the work, lift the bit often to clear chips, and let it cut.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether you resharpen once a year or replace bits every month.
