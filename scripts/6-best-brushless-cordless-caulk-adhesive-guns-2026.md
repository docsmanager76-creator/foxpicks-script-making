##HOOK
Search for a brushless glue gun and you will find a word that a hot glue gun cannot honestly use. A hot glue gun has no motor. The only tools in this area that can be brushless are the battery caulk and adhesive guns, the ones that push a tube of sealant for you.

So I went looking for proof. I read Bob Vila's hands-on test of cordless caulk guns, ToolGuyd's owner discussion, and the makers' own spec pages. Then I read the listings. Six guns made this list, and every one is judged on three questions in the same order: what the maker says about the motor, what a real tester found, and what it can't do. I'll tell you which one is number one and why.

Before that, one gun whose page sells a brushless motor and a brushed motor under the same title. Then a free step that saves you from the most common caulk gun complaint. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the SEESII CG120 Electric Caulking Gun.

This is for the person who wants the brushless motor the keyword promises, and is willing to take the seller's word for it.

It is the only gun here whose own listing says brushless. The page says the maker replaced the traditional brushed motor and claims three times the lifespan. It runs on 21 volts, comes with two two-thousand milliamp-hour batteries and a charger, and the listing says two full charges dispense about one hundred tubes of three hundred milliliters. It has stepless speed control, a ring-shaped LED light, an anti-drip function, and a ten ounce capacity. It weighs two point eight seven pounds, and the listing states a three year warranty.

Hold one thing: the three times lifespan is the seller's number. I found no test that measured it.

Now, the honest limits. I found no independent test of this gun at all. The brushless claim rests on the listing, and the listing has been up only since January 2026.

Its place in the lineup is the brushless claim.

Buy it if you want the lightest kit with batteries included. Do not buy it if you need a proven track record.

##P5
Number five, the CRAFTSMAN V20 Cordless Caulk Gun, model CMCE600B.

This is for someone who already owns V20 batteries and wants a plain, honest gun.

Here is the first surprise in this category. Craftsman's own product page states the motor type, and it is brushed. That one line is more than most listings give you. The same page lists five hundred fifty pounds of dispensing force, a variable speed dial with minimum and maximum settings, anti-drip that retracts the plunging rod, a cartridge holder that rotates three hundred sixty degrees, and a hang hook. It carries a three year limited warranty.

So the keyword's word is simply untrue for this one, and that is useful to know. A brushed motor is not a failure. It means the maker is being straight with you.

One small detail from that same page: the 20 volt MAX label is the battery's peak voltage with no load on it. The page itself says the nominal voltage is 18. That is how this whole tool class is labeled, so it is not a flaw in this gun, but it is worth knowing before you compare numbers.

Now, the honest limits. The listing gives about four and a half pounds, among the heavier guns here, and I found no independent test of it. It is a bare tool, so the battery and charger are extra.

Its place in the lineup is the honest brushed pick.

Buy it if you own V20 batteries. Do not buy it if you want a brushless motor.

Now, the honest limits. Beyond those headline figures, I found no independent test of this gun, and the listing is the lightest on detail of any here. It is sold as a tool only.

Its place in the lineup is the battery-platform pick.

Buy it if you already live in the ONE+ system. Do not buy it if you want a stated motor type.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the gun I promised. The Taskstar Electric Caulking Gun, listed with a brushless motor and two batteries.

The headline calls it upgraded brushless and promises a motor that runs quieter, stays cooler and lasts three times longer than standard electric caulking guns. It claims five hundred fifty pounds of thrust and about one hundred tubes on two batteries.

Then look at the size selector on the very same page. It offers a brushed motor with one battery, beside the brushless motor with two. One title, two different motors. A buyer who skims the headline can end up with the one the headline argues against.

The three times longer figure has no test behind it that I could find. To be fair, the brushless kit does include two batteries, which most of the name brands do not. That is the steel-man, and it is a real point. It just doesn't settle the motor question.

A word on a page is not a motor in your hand.

Buy number six if brushless matters to you. Otherwise, skip this one.

##FREE
Here is the free step, and it works with every gun here. The most common complaint in ToolGuyd's owner discussion was a gun struggling to push cold caulk. So bring the tube inside and let it come up to room temperature before you load it.

Then start slow. The SEESII listing tells you to raise the speed gradually when you use thicker products. That advice works on any of these guns, because a thick bead given too much speed makes a mess.

One more free habit before you buy any of these. Open the maker's spec page and look for the word motor. If it isn't there, the maker has not promised you anything about it, whatever a headline says.

Both habits cost nothing. They matter more than the motor type.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question. Does the page state the motor type? Only two of the six listings I read name one. The Craftsman says brushed. The SEESII says brushless, and nobody has tested the claim.

All seven links are in the description, including the Taskstar. Match the battery to the system you already own, check the push force against the material you use, and look for a stated warranty term.
