##HOOK
Every opener in this video promises the same thing: quiet. Ultra-quiet, whisper-quiet, made for a garage under a bedroom. So I read the maker's own listing for each one, then the roundups at This Old House and Family Handyman. Every listing I checked says quiet. Not one gives you a decibel number.

Quiet is the headline and nobody measures it, so I sorted six smart openers by what they actually state: motor, door limits, smart-home reach and warranty. I'll tell you which one is number one and why.

Before that, one pick that This Old House names its best value, and its own listing quietly walks the headline back. Then a free fix that quiets every opener here, whichever you buy. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the Chamberlain B3000 1/2 HP Belt Drive myQ Smart Garage Door Opener.

This is for a standard door in a garage that shares a wall with a bedroom, where you want quiet and a phone app and nothing more.

First, an honest flag. I found no independent test of this exact model, and neither This Old House nor Family Handyman reviews it, so everything here is the maker's own listing. It gives a half horsepower DC motor on a reinforced belt, battery backup, a one-button remote and a one-button wall control. It works with the HomeLink buttons in most cars made after 2016. It weighs thirty point four pounds, and the lighting takes one bulb, sold separately. The maker states five years on the motor and belt. Hold that number, because a pick near the top of this list prints two different ones on the same page.

Now, the honest limits. The app is Chamberlain's own myQ. In late 2023 Chamberlain blocked third-party apps from reaching it, which is why Home Assistant removed its myQ integration. So it works inside Chamberlain's world and with the partners it approves.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick, and the listing gives no door size.

Buy it if you want the simplest quiet opener. Do not buy it if you run Home Assistant.

##P5
Number five, the Genie QuietLift Connect 3053-TKV 3/4 HPc Belt Drive Smart Garage Door Opener.

This is for someone who wants voice control and a keypad in the box.

Same flag as number six: no independent test of this exact model. The listing gives a three-quarter HPc DC motor with a steel-reinforced belt. Notice Genie writes a small c after the letters. We'll come back to that. It lifts a sectional door up to seven feet tall and five hundred pounds, or eight feet with an extension kit sold separately. The rail snaps together in five pieces with no added hardware.

Next to number six, this one adds two pre-programmed three-button remotes, a wireless keypad and a multi-function wall console, where the Chamberlain ships a single one-button remote. Aladdin Connect is built in, and the listing says it works with Alexa and Google Assistant.

Now, the honest limits. The listing states no warranty term and no decibel number, and that seven foot, five hundred pound ceiling is a hard line. An eight-foot door needs the extension kit, and anything heavier needs a different pick. Number three covers that.

Its place in the lineup is the first step up from the entry pick, with the accessories already in the box.

Buy it if voice assistants matter and your door is standard height. Do not buy it for a tall or heavy door.

##P4
Number four, the Chamberlain RJO101 Wall Mount myQ Smart Garage Door Opener.

This is for a garage where the ceiling is taken, by a storage lift, a loft or low headroom.

No independent test of this one either. The listing says it mounts on the wall beside the door, so there is no overhead rail at all. It has built-in Wi-Fi with the myQ app, battery backup, a motion-sensing wall control and remote LED lighting. The maker describes a direct drive and says the design cuts noise and vibration, but again, no decibel number.

Here is a conflict I am not passing along. The listing says it supports doors up to thirty-six pounds, and the description says the opener itself weighs thirty-six pounds, while the spec table says thirty-three point six. That door figure is almost certainly the opener's own weight, mislabeled. So the listing never gives a real door limit. Ask Chamberlain before you buy.

Now, the honest limits. It carries the same myQ restriction as number six. The listing also states no warranty term and does not say which spring setups it fits.

Its place in the lineup is the specialist for a crowded ceiling.

Buy it if you want the rail gone and you will confirm the door weight first. Do not buy it on that listing alone.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The Craftsman CMXB4230 3/4 HP Belt Drive Smart Garage Door Opener.

This Old House names a Craftsman smart belt-drive opener its best value, and on paper the case is solid. It has a DC motor, a belt, battery backup and app control.

Now read the listing's own bullet. The title says three-quarter horsepower. The bullet says that figure is for comparison only, and that the lift power of DC motors is typically not measured in horsepower. This Old House also lists a ten-year motor warranty for its Craftsman pick, while this listing states five years on the motor and belt.

A horsepower rating that needs a disclaimer is a label, not a measurement.

If you want that app, buy number two. Otherwise, skip this one.

##P3
Number three, the Genie B6172H Wall Mount Smart Garage Door Opener.

This is for a tall or heavy door, and for anyone who wants the ceiling empty.

Where number four hides its door limit, this one prints it. A twenty-four volt DC motor lifts residential doors up to fourteen feet high and eight hundred fifty pounds, on standard, high-lift and vertical-lift doors. Number five stops at seven feet and five hundred. The listing states a fifteen-year motor warranty and five years on parts. It has battery backup, and it pulses the door before closing to check the cables for slack. Aladdin Connect is built in, and the listing names Alexa, Google Assistant, SmartThings and in-garage package delivery. It measures about seven inches wide, twelve tall and nine deep.

Now, the honest limits. The maker's own fine print says it will not work on reverse-wound springs, low-headroom outside hook-ups, extension springs or one-piece doors. It needs a standard outlet within six feet. So before anything else, look at your springs.

Its place in the lineup is the heavy-duty pick, and the only one with a stated door-weight limit this high.

Buy it if you have a torsion spring on a tall or heavy door. Do not buy it if your door runs on extension springs.

##P2
Number two, the Chamberlain B4310 3/4 HP Belt Drive myQ Smart Garage Door Opener with Camera.

This is for someone who wants to see the garage from their phone.

Now back to that warranty. This listing's bullet says five years on the motor and belt. Its spec table, on the same page, says a limited lifetime warranty on the motor and one year on parts. This Old House lists yet another set for its camera-equipped Chamberlain. Three versions of one promise, so I am giving you none of them. Ask before you buy.

What it does state is a three-quarter horsepower DC motor on a belt, and battery backup. The built-in camera gives a one hundred thirty degree view in the myQ app, the two lighting positions take LED bulbs sold separately, and two remotes come in the box. It weighs thirty point nine pounds.

So why isn't it number one? Because the camera is the only thing it adds. This Old House says that Chamberlain needs an app subscription for full function, and Family Handyman says camera recordings on a sibling Chamberlain need one too. And it still carries the myQ restriction.

Now, the honest limits. The warranty is unclear, the listing gives no door limit, and there is no decibel number.

Buy it if the camera matters. Do not buy it if you want recordings without a subscription.

##FREE
Here is the free fix, and it works on every opener here. Family Handyman says tighten every nut and bolt on the door and track hardware, then spray the rollers and hinges with garage door lube. Do not overtighten, because that can pull bolt heads through the door.

This Old House adds a rubber pad behind the wall bracket, so the hum of the opener stops traveling into the house.

Do both before you judge any opener's noise, because a rattling door will fool you. Tightening costs nothing. It quiets the noise that comes from the door, not the motor, and no listing can promise you that.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question. What does this listing say about my door? Height, weight and spring type. Most of the quiet in this category is promised, and the door limits are what the maker actually states.

All seven links are in the description, including the Craftsman. Check the door height and weight limits on each one, and look for a stated warranty term, because a few don't give one.
