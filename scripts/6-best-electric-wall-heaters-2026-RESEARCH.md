## FREE UPGRADE — the breaker check (replaces the CPSC recall)
NEC 424.4(B): fixed electric space-heating branch-circuit conductors are sized at 125% of the load (ECM, Mike Holt). Example: 3000 W / 240 V = 12.5 A × 1.25 = 15.6 A, so a 20 A circuit, matching Zoro's "min 20 A" for the 194. For plug-ins, maker instructions say no extension cords. Viewer action: read the heater's amps on its label, read the breaker number, and multiply by 1.25. If it doesn't fit, call the electrician before the heater, not after. The breaker vs. conductor debate (ECM vs. forum) is named, not hidden → "your electrician has the final say".

## MYTH — "the watts on the box are the watts you get"
Planted at #5 (Register's selectable 1600/900/700), paid at #1 (Energy Plus auto-sensing).
- Resistance heat scales with voltage squared: (208/240)² = 0.75. Listings confirm it: Cadet Twin 4000 W @240 → 3000 W @208; Broan 198 4000 → 3000 @208 (Crescent Electric / FirstSupply spec); Energy Plus 1600 / 1500 / 1000 at 240 / 208 / 120.
- Many apartments and condos run 208 V. Same heater, a quarter less heat.
- **Outlet conflict to name, not hide:** Bob Vila (Oct 2026) says the 198 "can also be attached to the wall and used at half-power through connection to a 120-volt outlet", and PickHVAC lists it as "120/240V". The maker's title and distributors list 240/208 V only; and a 240 V element on 120 V would make one quarter of its power, not half. Script line: "One big review site says…; the physics says…; the maker lists 240 or 208 only." (UNVERIFIED: Broan's own page returned 403; manual not read.)
- "Measured" proof is maker ratings, not a lab test — said openly.
