##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any wrench on this list.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not a lab test.

First, use impact sockets, the black ones built for impact tools. Bob Vila warns that chrome sockets are not made for impact work.

Second, start every lug by hand, so it does not cross-thread.

Third, snug lugs with the auto-stop or lowest setting. An impact wrench is not a torque wrench.

Fourth, finish with a torque wrench to the spec in your owner's manual. Honda says to work in a star pattern and go around two to three times.

Fifth, check the torque again after the first short drive.

Sixth, keep the hog ring or friction ring clean. A worn ring drops sockets.

##TAIL
Before you buy, ask one question: where will this wrench spend its life? Lug nuts and brake jobs on a car need control more than torque. Rusted suspension bolts on a truck need the big numbers. And tight engine bays need a short head.
