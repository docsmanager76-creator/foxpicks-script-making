## 3. Buyer checklist (the spec-matrix columns)
1. Drive type: manual lever, manual pistol grip, air, or cordless (and which battery platform)
2. Maximum pressure in PSI (for clogged zerks), and the risk it poses to trailer seals
3. Flow rate (oz/min) or output per stroke
4. Hose length and reach (loader booms, axle pivots)
5. Cartridge size (standard 14 oz vs 3 oz) and bulk-fill or suction-fill support
6. Coupler type (standard 4-jaw vs locking)
7. Air bleeder or priming valve
8. Weight and one- or two-hand use
9. Extras: LED light, speed settings, grease counter, foot or stand
10. What's in the box (battery, charger, hose, strap, case)
11. Warranty
12. Recommended uses

## 5. Hook — type 4, Contradiction
- **Material.** Almost every cordless gun on the shelf leads with the same number, 10,000 PSI. SlashGear (Aug 2025) says that for trailer wheel bearings, lower pressure and slower delivery are what keep you from over-greasing. Over-greasing "can cause bearing seal failure" and puts grease onto the braking surfaces.
- etrailer: overfilled hubs push grease out through the seal's rubber lip.
- Tools in Action, meanwhile, called a 10,000 PSI cordless gun a must-have on the farm.
- **The contradiction.** The tractor wants force and the trailer wants restraint, and one garage holds both.
- Open loops:
  1. which gun handles both
  2. the best-selling gun in the category that holds a fraction of a normal cartridge (villain)
  3. the free habit that matters more than PSI (free upgrade)

## 6. Myth — "more PSI = a better gun"
- DeWalt DCGG571M1 and Ryobi P3410 are both rated 10,000 PSI, yet the makers list 5.0 vs 7.5 oz/min, so the same PSI gives a 50% difference in flow.
- LockNLube is rated only 5,000 PSI, half the cordless figure. Its locking coupler is what owners praise (forums, plus owner summary: "fantastic locking mechanism").
- PSI only matters for breaking a clogged zerk. For trailer hubs, more is a liability (SlashGear, etrailer).
- These figures are maker claims, not measured. The script should say "rated".

## 7. Villain — type B, Hype/viral: KRETLAW K180006 3 oz Mini Grease Gun
- **Hype.** The most-bought gun in the category right now (INTERNAL: #1 in node, 4K+), and its own listing names tractors, PTO axles and steering cylinders.
- **Specific failures (sourced):**
  - **Capacity.** It holds 3 oz cartridges against the standard 14 oz (listing). A TractorByNet owner says one PTO shaft can take 25–30 pumps every 8 hours, so tractor-scale greasing means constant reloads.
  - **Pressure.** It is rated 3,000 PSI, under a third of the cordless picks and half of the FIXSMITH. That is weak for a seized zerk.
  - **Owner reports.** Owners describe grease leaking out the side and through the handle spring, and several say it broke by the third use. These come from the listing's customer summary; in the script say "owners report" and give no counts.
- **Fair framing.** It is fine for a mower deck or a garage cart, but it is the wrong tool for a tractor or trailer hubs.
- **Link:** https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D67HJBWW
- **Weakness of this villain.** No outlet reviewed it; the failure evidence is owner reports plus spec math. Backup villain: Milwaukee M12 2446-20, type D. SlashGear says some users report it could do better on power and battery life; it carries 7 cartridges per charge.

## 8. Callbacks
- **C1.** FIXSMITH (#6) gives 1 oz per 40 strokes, so a 14 oz cartridge takes about 560 strokes by hand. DeWalt (#1) is rated 5 oz per minute, which means that same cartridge empties in under 3 minutes of trigger time.
- **C2.** Lincoln 1162 (#3) has the pneumatic's tether: it needs a compressor and an air line, so it is a shop gun, not a field gun. Milwaukee M18 (#2) gives continuous powered flow with no hose to the compressor, plus a longer 48" hose (vs 30") and a grease counter, which also answers the trailer over-greasing worry.

## 9. Free upgrade (~127–130 words in the script)
- **Wipe, purge, wipe** (Mobil regreasing guidance, plus Machinery Lubrication on grease "dollops"):
  1. Wipe the zerk before you attach the coupler, so you do not pump the grit in.
  2. Pump until fresh grease purges at the joint.
  3. Wipe off the excess, because a dollop left on the fitting collects dirt that the next service pushes into the bearing.
- **Trailer twist.** On hubs with a Bearing Buddy-type cap, do not pump to purge. Stop when the cap's piston starts to move (etrailer / forum consensus).
