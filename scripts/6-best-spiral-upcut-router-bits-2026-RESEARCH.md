## 2. Editorial reality
- **No independent outlet has tested a spiral upcut bit against others.** Searched Bob Vila, Fine Woodworking, Popular Mechanics, Pro Tool Reviews, Wood Magazine, Popular Woodworking: only Bob Vila and Fine Woodworking returned anything usable.
- **Bob Vila**, "Best Router Bits" (published 2020-07-02, updated 2025-10-17; Mark Wolfe, Timothy Dale; hands-on, 77 bits from seven makers): names ONE upcut spiral, the **Whiteside RU5150** (1/2 in shank), as "Best for Cutting Circles". Cuts cleanly in any grain direction, clears chips from the groove like a drill bit, clean edges in oak, pine, and plywood. Con: it costs more than a two-flute straight bit. The page does not say which author tested it. None of the six picks is covered.
- **Fine Woodworking**, Patrick Warner, "Spiral Router Bits vs. Straight Router Bits" (#140, Jan/Feb 2000; paywalled after an excerpt): upcut pushes chips up toward the shank, downcut pushes them down; spirals leave a cleaner edge and spread the cut over a longer edge; mostly solid carbide; sharpening is hard. No product names. 26 years old.
- **Maker/retailer claims** (not tests): Freud (Black I.C.E. coating, "2X increased life vs standard uncoated bits", lifetime limited warranty, good for mortises and deep blind holes, best quality on the bottom surface); Whiteside (clean edge on the side of the material farthest from the router; rapid chip removal in deep cuts); Whiteside warranty text on retailer pages: defects in workmanship and materials, repair or replace.
- **Retailer guides** (Infinity Tools, KM Tools, CNC Router Store): upcut lifts fibers at the top edge and can chip out on plywood and veneers; downcut or compression suit those. This is consistent but is vendor material, used only as a flagged claim.
- Content farms and affiliate pages (fypower, journeymanhq, pixelfy, foliumbiosciences, mirror/listing sites) EXCLUDED, including their "winners".
- OUTLET GAP: all six picks are maker and listing claims; the only outlet that tested an upcut spiral tested the villain. Say so openly.

## 3. Buyer checklist
1. Upcut vs downcut vs compression — which face stays clean (upcut: the bottom, the face away from the router; the top can tear out).
2. Cutting diameter (1/8, 1/4, 1/2 in) = groove width.
3. Shank size matches your collet (1/4 in vs 1/2 in).
4. Cutting length vs depth of cut; overall length for reach.
5. Flute count (2 flute standard; 3 flute for removal rate).
6. Coating (Black I.C.E., nACo, none) and material (solid carbide).
7. Router fit: hand-held, router table, CNC; speed range.
8. Warranty term.
9. Plunge-capable (bottom-cutting) for mortises and blind slots.
10. Sold singly vs sets.

## 5. Hook — type 3 Reader's Pain
You set a spiral upcut bit in your router to cut a groove in plywood and the top face splinters along the edge. Here is what I found: an upcut spiral lifts the fibers at the top edge by design, and only the bottom face stays clean. Whiteside's own listing says the clean edge is on the side farthest from the router. Pick the wrong bit for the job and you ruin the part. Open loops: villain, free upgrade (the scrap test), which bit fits which cut.

## 6. Myth
"An upcut spiral is the clean-cut bit." It is the chip-clearing bit; the clean face is the bottom. Diameters run 1/8 to 1/4 in here, cutting lengths 1/2 to 2 in, flute counts 2 to 3.

## 7. Villain — type C Overpriced Premium (famous name): Whiteside RU5150
The one upcut spiral an outlet actually tested (Bob Vila, Best for Cutting Circles, clean in oak, pine, plywood). The only con Bob Vila names is that it costs more than a two-flute straight bit. Not a pick: it is a 1/2 in cut on a 1/2 in shank, so it needs a 1/2 in collet and a 1/2 in wide groove, and the rest of this lineup is 1/8 to 1/4 in work. ## 9. Free upgrade
No purchase: (1) cut a groove in a scrap of your own plywood, upcut vs flip the workpiece, see which face tears; (2) match the shank to your collet, check the collet is clean and the bit sits at least three-quarters of the shank deep; (3) set the router speed to the bit's size; (4) take light passes and cut deep grooves in two or three; (5) put the good face down, and press painter's tape along the top edge; (6) wipe pitch off the bit with a cleaner.
