##HOOK
Search for a cordless brushless flooring nailer and you are searching for a category that does not exist. Every flooring nailer on this list runs on compressed air. Powernail's own product page says to use a compressor between seventy and one hundred ten PSI. Bob Vila's tester connected an air hose to the tool and fired it by tapping with a mallet. And Woodsmith, in a 2021 roundup, described one maker's flooring stapler as battery and fuel cell with no compressor, while the maker's own listing says it is air powered. So the label and the machine disagree. Only one tool here was tested on a real floor, and I will tell you which. Six flooring nailers made the list, I will warn you off one more, and number one comes last. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any nailer on this list.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not a lab test.

First, match the base plate to the thickness of your flooring, and check the plate range in your tool's manual.

Second, use cleats over a plywood subfloor and staples over OSB, which is what a forum of installers recommends.

Third, screw the subfloor down first to stop squeaks, because a loose subfloor will show through the new floor.

Fourth, keep your compressor inside the maker's pressure range. Powernail says seventy to one hundred ten PSI for its tool.

Fifth, test on a scrap board and set the depth before you start a row.

Sixth, strike the mallet squarely, and wear the safety glasses.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this claim come from a test or from a label? Cordless brushless is a label. Finished the whole floor and never jammed came from Bob Vila's test. Whether it needs a compressor is not on most boxes, either.

All seven links are in the description, including the Powernail. Check your flooring thickness, your subfloor, the weight, and whether you already own a compressor. Then read your flooring maker's instructions before you fire a single cleat.
