##P5
Number five, the Dyna-Glo IR30NMDG-1 30,000 BTU Natural Gas Infrared Wall Heater.

This is for a garage, a workshop, or a drafty room where you want to feel heat fast.

Here is the myth I promised. The description calls these heaters ninety nine point nine percent efficient. That number is real, and it is not a reason to buy. A vent-free heater keeps nearly all its heat because it keeps nearly all its exhaust. The Department of Energy says unvented units put water vapor, nitrogen oxides, and carbon monoxide into your living space, and the vapor can feed mold. Ninety nine point nine percent means everything stays in the room. Everything.

What you get here is infrared. Ceramic plaques glow and warm objects and people first, the way the sun does. Dyna-Glo rates it at thirty thousand B T U for up to one thousand square feet. A low, medium, high knob sets the output, and it needs no electricity, so it keeps working in an outage. An optional fan and floor legs are sold separately. It weighs twenty five pounds, and the warranty is one year.

Now, the honest limits. There is no thermostat, because the knob sets a flame, not a temperature. And it is not sold in California or Canada.

Its place in the lineup is the value pick.

Buy it for a cold workshop, skip it if you want set and forget heat.

Its place in the lineup is the specialist pick, with a two year limited warranty.

Buy it for a bedroom, skip it for an interior wall.

##FREE
The free upgrade is about your room, not your heater.

Every vent-free heater burns the air you breathe, so measure the room before you install one. Multiply length by width by ceiling height. The national fuel gas code, N F P A fifty four, calls a space confined when it has less than fifty cubic feet for every thousand B T U burning in it. For a thirty thousand B T U heater, that is fifteen hundred cubic feet, about a fourteen foot square room with eight foot ceilings. Put a carbon monoxide alarm in that room and test it. The Consumer Product Safety Commission adds one habit: keep a window open at least an inch while it runs.

That check costs nothing, and it decides whether the room can hold the heater.

##TAIL
Before you buy, ask one question: where does the exhaust go? A vent-free heater keeps it in your room, and a direct vent sends it outside. Independent testing of these heaters is almost nonexistent, so most numbers here are the makers' own, and I said where.

All the product links are in the description. On each one, check the fuel, because natural gas and propane models are sold separately and most cannot convert. And check whether your city allows vent-free heaters. California has banned them.
