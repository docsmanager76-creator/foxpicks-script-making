## 3. Buyer checklist
1. What the pipe is (metal or plastic) and whether the cable says it fits plastic.
2. Self-regulating (output changes with temperature, overlap allowed) vs constant-wattage-style (do not wrap or overlap) — follow the maker's rule.
3. Length that matches the run (plus the power cord), and whether it can be cut.
4. Watts per foot (3, 5, 7).
5. Built-in thermostat on/off temperatures (37 to 43 F on, 50 to 55 F off).
6. Minimum rating (-40 F listed on most).
7. Safety listing: UL, ETL, CSA, or CE only; the whole cable vs the plug only.
8. Plug with indicator light and grounded plug; GFCI outlet.
9. Tape and insulation rules; never on a leaking pipe; keep off flammables.
10. Warranty term.
11. Indoor only vs outdoor jacket.

## 5. Hook — type 4 Contradiction
Frost King's own instruction sheet says its cable must not be wrapped or spiraled and must lie flat. Others here, HEATIT among them, say overlap and spiral are fine. Same job, opposite rules. Wrong rule on the wrong cable, and you risk overheating. Open loops: villain, free upgrade, which rule applies to which cable.

## 6. Myth

## 9. Free upgrade
Measure the pipe run before ordering; check the pipe material; seal air leaks and add insulation (half-inch fiberglass or foam) over the cable only as the maker allows; test the cable now: ice pack on the thermostat for twenty minutes (not in warm rooms); plug into a GFCI outlet; check the indicator light; inspect for frays and leaks; keep flammables clear; open the cabinet door under the sink on freezing nights; let a faucet drip. No purchase needed.
