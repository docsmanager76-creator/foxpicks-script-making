##HOOK
Every utility faucet listing sells you the same promise: a threaded spout, so you can screw on a hose. But one faucet on this list says, in its own title, three quarter inch N P T. A garden hose is three quarter inch G H T. One has fourteen threads per inch and a taper. The other has eleven and a half, and runs straight. Gates, a big hose fitting maker, says they are not compatible. And the spec sheet for Bob Vila's best overall utility faucet warns that local code may require a backflow preventer. So this countdown checks the thread, the reach, and the warranty on six faucets. There is a famous commercial faucet that costs you warranty years. And there is a free fix that keeps a bucket of bleach out of your pipes. All the product links are in the description.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick with everything in the box.

Buy it for a quick weekend swap, skip it if a hose is your main job.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade for any threaded spout.

First, read the thread. A garden hose is G H T. If the listing says N P T, buy an adapter, not a bigger wrench. Second, before any hose goes on, add a hose connection vacuum breaker, the small brass cap rated A S S E ten eleven. Here is why. If your water pressure drops while a hose sits in a bucket of bleach, the line can siphon that bucket back toward your pipes. Code guidance from the I C C and state health agencies calls for it on threaded spouts. Third, never leave the hose end sitting in the bucket. Check your local code.

Reading the thread and lifting the hose cost nothing, and they keep bleach out of your drinking water.
