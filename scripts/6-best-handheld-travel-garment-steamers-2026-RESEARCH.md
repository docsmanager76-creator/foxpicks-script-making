## 3. Buyer checklist
1. Voltage: 120V only vs 100-240V. A plug adapter does not change voltage.
2. Weight and folded size for the carry-on.
3. Tank size and continuous run time (ml / minutes).
4. Heat-up time (seconds vs minutes).
5. Steam vs steam-plus-plate (can it press a collar?).
6. Leak behavior: tilt, upside down, spit after use.
7. Cord length.
8. Auto shut-off.
9. What is in the box (bag, brush, heat cover).
10. Cool-down before packing.
11. Water: distilled vs tap (Nori states distilled helps; others silent).
12. Warranty term.

## 5. Hook — type 6 Category Lie
"Travel steamer" says nothing about where you can plug it in. Five of the six picks here are 120V only; some listings warn that plugging into 240V, even with an adapter, can damage them. Only one pick is dual voltage. Open loops: villain (famous name), the one dual-voltage pick, free upgrade (check the label before you pack). Check: no outlet tested the dual-voltage pick — said openly.

## 6. Myth
"A travel steamer works anywhere you can plug it in." The plug adapter changes the shape, not the voltage. Sizes here run 1.2 to 2.4 lb, tanks 90 to 240 ml, heat-up 15 seconds to 2 minutes.

## 9. Free upgrade
No purchase: (1) read the label on the steamer for "100-240V"; (2) never plug a 120V steamer into 240V through an adapter; (3) check the hotel outlet and any adapter are rated for the wattage (listing warning on OGHom); (4) fill, heat, then steam from the bottom up and hang the garment; (5) let it cool fully before packing (Reemix 17 min per TODAY); (6) steam in the bathroom with the door closed if no hanger (unverified home tip — OMIT unless sourced); (7) test on a hidden seam first.
