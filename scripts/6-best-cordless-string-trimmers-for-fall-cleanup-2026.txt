##HOOK
One hundred forty minutes. Twenty minutes. Same lab, same test, both battery string trimmers. TechGearLab ran those numbers with the head spinning and nothing in front of it, and it warns that real weeds pull them down.

Now picture your fall cleanup. Dead stalks along the fence, tall grass you gave up on in August, and a battery that quits with half the yard left. Bob Vila ran twenty-two trimmers through more than forty hours of testing, and it warned that even a forty-five minute trimmer may fall short for seasonal cleanup.

Six trimmers made this list. I'll show you which one finishes the job, why voltage is the wrong number to shop by, and which popular trimmer I'd skip. Then there's one free habit that protects your battery all winter. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the Craftsman CMCST901M1 13-inch V20 String Trimmer and Edger Kit.

This is for a small yard where fall cleanup means tidy edges and a fence line, not a jungle.

It is the simplest way in, because the box holds the trimmer, a spool of point zero six five line, a four amp-hour battery and a charger. The swath is thirteen inches, so a long fence takes extra passes. Two speed settings let you favor power or runtime. The head turns to edge, so one tool handles the sidewalk too, and the handle adjusts to your height.

Craftsman claims up to sixty-three minutes, and its fine print says that is on low speed. TechGearLab measured a different V20, a fifteen inch Weedwacker, at thirty-five minutes with no load, and it was the quietest they tested at eighty-one and a half decibels. Hold that gap, because number one shows you a much smaller one.

Now, the honest limits. That lab says the V20 it tested is not powerful enough for thick weeds and shrubs, and is better at maintaining a yard than reclaiming one. One retailer lists this model as brushed, and the fifty-two percent more power on the listing is measured against Craftsman's own CMCST915.

One retailer lists a three-year limited warranty. Its place in the lineup is the entry point. Buy it for a kept lawn, and skip it if your fence line grows saplings.

##P5
Number five, the Black+Decker LST136B 13-inch 40V MAX String Trimmer and Lawn Edger, tool only.

This is for someone who cares most about clean driveway edges and already owns a Black+Decker forty volt battery.

TechGearLab named the LST136 kit its Best Bang for Your Buck, scored it seventy-three, and praised its pivoting head for edging. They list that kit at six point six pounds. It has an automatic feed spool, so you never bump it, and a power dial that trades strength for runtime.

Now, the myth. That lab lists this forty volt MAX tool at thirty-six volts nominal. Their fifty-six volt, four amp-hour trimmer ran fifty minutes. A thirty-six volt, five amp-hour trimmer ran eighty-eight. A forty volt, six amp-hour trimmer ran one hundred forty. More volts did not mean more minutes. You are buying a whole battery and motor, not a number on the box.

Now, the honest limits. This listing has no battery and no charger, and the forty-three minute runtime came from the two amp-hour kit. The review also disagrees with itself: the text says it did remarkably well on thicker weeds, while the cons list says it struggles with dense weeds. Changing the line means taking the head off.

In the box you get the spool, the guard and an auxiliary handle, and the listing gives a three-year limited warranty. Buy it if you own the battery, and skip it if you don't.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The EGO ST1502SA 15-inch Rapid Reload string trimmer.

Here is the steel-man. Reviewed named its close sibling, the ST1500-S, a best battery pick, though it did not test this exact model. TechGearLab says this one excels in high weeds and calls it well balanced. On paper, that sounds like a fall trimmer.

Now the problem. That same lab scored it fifty-nine out of one hundred. It ran thirty minutes with no load on its two and a half amp-hour pack. It flattens grass instead of cutting it, and it has no strap attachment for a long job. Reviewed also saw the sibling's cutting drop noticeably as the string frayed.

A trimmer that flattens grass is a lawn roller with extra steps.

Same brand, same lab, number one scored eighty-four, so buy that instead. Keep this one for short runs through high weeds, or skip it.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every trimmer here. Your battery's enemy this winter is not the cold alone, it is how you put it away.

When the last leaves are done, take the pack off the tool. Consumer Reports says that if you won't use it for thirty days or more, charge or run it down to roughly forty percent. Store it indoors, because most batteries do best between forty and eighty degrees.

STIHL's guide says forty to sixty percent, and one EGO dealer says thirty to fifty, so your manual gets the final word. Then wipe the clippings off with a damp cloth.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether you buy a new pack in spring or keep the one you own.
