##HOOK
Almost every sander box in this class prints a horsepower number. So I did the math on it. One horsepower is about seven hundred and forty-six watts. So three-quarter horsepower means about five hundred and sixty watts coming out of the motor. Now plug a four-point-three-amp motor into a normal hundred-and-twenty-volt outlet. At most, it draws about five hundred and sixteen watts going in. No motor puts out more than it takes in. Yet listings I checked claim three-quarter horsepower from four point three amps, from four point five, and from five. So read the amps, not the horsepower. I read Bob Vila's bench sander test, checked every listing, and kept the amps honest. Six sanders made the list, I will warn you off one more, and number one comes last. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any sander on this list.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not a lab test.

First, bolt the sander to your bench through the holes in the base.

Second, hook a shop vacuum to the dust port, and still wear a mask.

Third, sand only on the disc's down-spinning half, so the work is pushed onto the table.

Fourth, use light pressure and keep the piece moving, or you will burn the wood and sand past your line.

Fifth, after every belt change, run it briefly and center the belt with the tracking knob.

Sixth, clean a clogged belt with a rubber cleaning stick, and keep a separate belt for metal, because sparks and sawdust do not mix.
