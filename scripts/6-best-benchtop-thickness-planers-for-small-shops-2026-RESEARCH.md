## Buyer-decision checklist
1. Weight and footprint (55 to 102 lb) and whether it needs a stand or bench bolt-down. 2. Cutterhead: straight knives (2 or 3) vs spiral. 3. Snipe control: carriage lock, extension tables. 4. Max cut per pass (about 1/8 in), speeds (one or two feed speeds). 5. Width (12 to 13 in) and thickness (6 in). 6. Dust collection: port size, fan-assist, is the port included. 7. Noise (dB). 8. Warranty. 9. Knife change ease and spare knives. 10. Power: all are 15 A, needs its own circuit.

## Hook (recommend type 3 Reader's Pain; types 1 to 5 available)
Snipe: the last few inches of every board come out thinner, and in a small shop you are the one who has to lift 60 to 100 lb to the bench. Open loops: villain, free upgrade. No pick brands.

## Myth
"More power means a better planer." Bob Vila's table shows every pick is a 15 A motor; the difference is cutterhead, snipe control, feed speed and weight. Cited as spec arithmetic.

## Free upgrade
1) Support long boards at both ends (roller stand or extension) and feed the full board length. 2) Keep shavings off the table (Woodgears). 3) Take the last passes light and bolt down the light machines (Bob Vila on Craftsman and WEN). 4) Plug into its own 15 A circuit and run a vacuum on the dust port. Verify every step against the sources.
