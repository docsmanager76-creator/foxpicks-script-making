##HOOK
TechGearLab tested thirteen jump starters on vehicles up to a six point seven litre V8 diesel. They bypassed the truck's own battery so the pack did all the work, and measured how much energy each one really held. The best delivered ninety three percent of its claim, and the worst delivered fifty seven. Car and Driver ran seven more on dead-battery cars.

Here is what is coming. The amp number printed biggest on every box, and why a company that makes one of these six calls it unregulated. The jump starter most people are told to buy, and why its own maker rates it for half a diesel. I'll name it before the top three. And a habit from a manufacturer's manual that keeps any of them ready in January.

The six best jump starters for diesel and V8 engines of 2026. Product links are in the description.
##P6
Number six, the DeWalt DXAEJ14 1600 Peak Amp Jump Starter with 120 PSI Air Inflator.

This is the shop box, for a garage where a flat tyre is as likely as a flat battery. DeWalt rates it for four, six and eight cylinder gas and diesel engines, with 1,600 peak amps from a lead-acid battery, and it works with wet, gel and AGM vehicle batteries.

The inflator is the reason to buy it. Set a target pressure up to a hundred and twenty psi and it stops on its own, with a ten minute auto shut-off in case you walk away. You also get a 25 watt USB-C port, a 15 watt USB-A port, an LED area light and a reverse polarity warning. It recharges from a standard extension cord.

TechGearLab made it a top pick and weighed it at seventeen and a half pounds.

Now, the honest limits. DeWalt publishes no litre rating and no cranking amps. And TechGearLab measured a six hour recharge and wrote that it is not meant for repeated consecutive boosts.

Buy it for the garage wall. Skip it if it has to live in the truck.

Six hours to recharge. Hold that, because number one is ready again in an hour and a half.
##P5
Number five, the Clore Automotive Jump-N-Carry JNC660 1700 Peak Amp Jump Starter.

Here is the myth, and Clore breaks it themselves. On their own product page this unit is listed at 1,700 peak amps and 425 cranking amps. Peak is a split second burst, cranking is current held long enough to turn an engine, and one number is four times the other. Almost every other box on this shelf prints only the big one.

What makes this the professional's classic is the cables. Forty six inch, number two gauge welding cable, the thickest in this video, with industrial clamps. With cable, a lower gauge number means thicker wire, and thicker wire delivers more of the current to a big diesel's terminals.

Inside is a 22 amp hour AGM Proformer battery. There is a voltmeter showing its charge, a 12 volt outlet for accessories, and a built-in automatic charger. Clore backs it for one year, and TechGearLab put it through their thirteen unit test.

Now, the honest limits. Clore does not publish an engine size rating for it. It weighs eighteen pounds by Clore's own figure, and there is no USB charging for your phone.

Buy it for a service truck or a farm shop. Skip it if you need it in a glovebox.
##P4
Number four, the NOCO Boost X GBX75 2500A Jump Starter.

This is lithium in a size you can keep in the cab, and NOCO rates it for 8.5 litre gas and 6.5 litre diesel engines at 2,500 peak amps, which covers most V8 pickups and a lot of diesels.

NOCO's UltraSafe 2.0 system is spark-proof with reverse polarity protection. When a battery is so flat the sensors cannot see it, below three volts, you hold manual override for three seconds and it forces the start. NOCO quotes up to twelve jump starts per charge.

Charging is the modern part. It takes and gives sixty watts over USB-C, so it recharges in under two hours and charges a laptop in reverse. NOCO says five minutes on the charger is enough for a boost, even from empty. Add a 400 lumen light with seven modes and heavy-duty clamps, under a one year warranty.

Now, the honest limits. NOCO does not publish its battery capacity. TechGearLab tested its bigger sibling, the GBX155, not this model. And six and a half litres of diesel stops just short of the six point seven litre diesels in a lot of heavy pickups.

Buy it for a V8 or a smaller diesel. Skip it if you drive a six point seven.

A great tool for the wrong engine is still the wrong tool.

##P3
Number three, the Gooloo GT6000 6000A Jump Starter.

Car and Driver made this their best for large engines, and it has the biggest numbers in the video. Gooloo claims 6,000 peak amps, every gas engine and diesels up to twelve litres, and a 27,000 milliamp hour battery.

The charging is the best here. A hundred watt, two-way USB-C port takes it from empty to full in 1.4 hours, and Gooloo says seven minutes of charging is enough for one start. The same port will run a laptop. There is also a 15 volt, 10 amp DC outlet for a tyre inflator or a cooler, a 3.2 inch status display, ten safety protections and a 400 lumen light.

Gooloo claims a twenty four month standby time, and the box includes heavy duty jumper cables, a 100 watt car charger and a storage bag.

Now, the honest limits, and they are about the missing half of the spec sheet. Gooloo publishes no cranking amps, no cable gauge and no weight. Hold on to that, because the next machine publishes all three.

So why isn't it higher? Because it prints the biggest number on this shelf and leaves out the ones that predict a cold start.
##P2
Number two, the Clore Automotive Jump-N-Carry JNC325 2000A Lithium Jump Starter.

This is Car and Driver's best overall, and it is where the myth finishes. The listing calls it a 2000 amp jump starter. Clore's own product page rates the same unit at 450 start assist amps. Same product, same company, two numbers, and the honest one won the test.

Now compare it with number three. Gooloo claims more than twice the peak amps and publishes no cable gauge. Clore tells you exactly what you are getting: twenty four inch, number six gauge cables with full size clamps, which Clore itself points out other lithium units do not use.

The rest of the spec sheet is complete too. It is rated for eight litre gas and six litre diesel engines. An override button will start a battery sitting at zero point zero volts. It carries reverse polarity, backfeed, over-voltage and overheat protection, and meets the UL 2743 standard for jump starters. You get two USB outlets, a 10 amp DC outlet and an LED light. It recharges over USB-C, with the charger and a case included, weighs 4.3 pounds and carries a one year warranty.

Now, the honest limits. Clore does not publish battery capacity or recharge time. The twenty four inch cables are short on a tall truck. And six litres of diesel, like number four, stops below the six point sevens.

Buy it for honest numbers in a four pound package. Skip it for heavy duty diesels.
##P1
Number one, the Wolfbox MegaVolt24 MV24 4000A Jump Starter.

This is TechGearLab's editors' choice, and the reason is measured, not claimed. Wolfbox says 88.8 watt hours and TechGearLab measured 82.3, which is ninety three percent, the closest to its claim of anything in their test. On a V6 it started three times in a row using about three percent of its battery each time.

It is rated for ten litre gas and ten litre diesel engines, which covers the six point seven litre diesels that numbers two and four fall short of. Peak current is 4,000 amps, and there is a one-touch boost for vehicles too flat to register.

Wolfbox quotes a full recharge in 1.3 hours on its 65 watt USB-C port, and TechGearLab measured an hour and a half. That port and a QC 3.0 USB-A port both charge your devices. Add an LCD status screen, an LED light with SOS and strobe modes, an operating range from minus four to 140 degrees Fahrenheit, IP64 weather sealing, and UL, FCC and CE certification. TechGearLab weighed it at 3.2 pounds.

And here is the callback. Wolfbox's own buying guide says peak amps is an unregulated marketing claim with no universal test standard. Their box still says 4,000. Judge it by what TechGearLab measured instead.

Now, the honest limits, both from the testers. The clamps are stubby, and the flashlight is mediocre.

Buy this if one jump starter has to cover every engine in the driveway. That's why it's number one.
##FREEUPGRADE
Now the free habit, and it works on every jump starter here.

Clore's manual for the JNC660 says it plainly. Charge it for twenty four hours when it comes out of the box. Charge it after every use. Charge it before you put it away.

That matters because TechGearLab tested these packs partly depleted, in freezing weather. That is how yours will be found on the one morning you need it.

Then the clamps. The same manual says store them in their holsters, away from each other and anything metal. If they touch, the battery can short and get hot enough to ignite most materials.

Charged on a schedule, clamps apart, and no money involved.
