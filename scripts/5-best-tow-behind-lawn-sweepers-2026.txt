##HOOK
It rained after you mowed, and now the clippings are wet. That is exactly when the sweeper you bought for a big lawn can become the thing that won't move.

Bob Vila hitched several tow-behind sweepers to a 22-horsepower riding mower, let a rain shower soak the clippings, and swept. The biggest hopper in the test got too heavy to tow uphill once it was half full.

So if you have read sweeper listings all week and every one says bigger is better, this fixes that.

Five tow-behind sweepers made this list, all built for a riding mower or tractor. I'll show you which one handles a wet lawn, why hopper size is the wrong number to shop by, and the one free habit that matters more than either. All the product links are in the description.

##P5
Number five, the Brinly 42-Inch Tow-Behind Lawn Sweeper in Hammered Finish with High-Velocity Brushes.

This is for the owner of a riding mower who wants a wide sweep path and a bag that dumps without climbing off the seat.

I found no outlet that tested a Brinly sweeper, so everything here is the maker's claim. The listing gives a forty-two inch sweep width and a twenty cubic foot hamper that swings open on a pull cord with a T-handle. Six high-velocity brushes turn at a five to one gear ratio, which is meant to flick leaves off the ground, even through tractor turns.

Brush height locks with a twist, so a bump over uneven ground should not change it. A removable mesh windscreen keeps light debris from blowing out of the bag. The hamper braces collapse, so it stands upright in a corner of the barn. It weighs about sixty-eight pounds.

Remember that gear ratio. The Agri-Fab at number one runs five point six to one.

Now, the honest limits. This listing names no warranty, no tire size and no hitch type, so ask before you buy. A full hamper of wet clippings is also heavy however well it is built.

Its place in the lineup is the entry point. Buy it for leaves and clippings on a mowed lawn. Skip it if you want a machine with an outlet's verdict behind it.

##P4
Number four, the Brinly LS2-42BH-S 42-Inch Tow-Behind Lawn Sweeper in Gunmetal Finish with Universal Hitch.

This is for the same buyer as number five, who wants a model number on the listing.

Yes, this is the same sweeper in a different color. The bullets are word for word the same: forty-two inch width, twenty cubic foot hamper, six brushes, twist-lock height and a mesh windscreen. The differences are a title that says universal hitch and a listed weight of seventy point four pounds. I am keeping it as its own pick because the model number lets you match a manual or a part.

Now, the myth. Bigger hopper means better. The testers' mower was twenty-two horsepower. Their twelve cubic foot Agri-Fab, number two here, towed wet clippings without a struggle. Their twenty-five cubic foot Agri-Fab, number one, was too heavy to pull uphill at half full. Your mower, not the bag, sets how much you can haul. You are buying a bag size, not a finished lawn.

The twenty cubic feet on this Brinly sits between those two bags.

Now, the honest limits. I found no outlet that tested it, and the listing shows no warranty. The title says universal hitch, but the bullets never say which tractors it fits, so check your drawbar.

Its place in the lineup is the model-numbered twin. Buy it to match parts. Skip it if you already bought number five.

##P3
Number three, the Ohio Steel 4222V2 42-Inch Tow-Behind Lawn Sweeper with 22 Cu. Ft. Hopper.

This is for a lot full of oak leaves and acorns, where bristles take a beating.

Be clear on the model. Bob Vila named a fifty inch, twenty-six cubic foot Ohio Steel its Best Heavy-Duty pick, and I could not find that one to link. This is the forty-two inch member of the family, with the maker's patented spiral polypropylene brushes, eleven inches across. The testers called the fifty inch excellent on acorns, pine cones and other dense debris.

The listing gives a twenty-two cubic foot bag, eleven inch semi-pneumatic wheels and sealed ball bearings on the brush axle. The ratio is four point five to one, lower than number one. The drawbar offsets left or right so you can mow and sweep in one pass, and the drawbar and bag fold for storage. The maker says it assembles in thirty minutes with one tool.

Now, the honest limits. The testers called the fifty inch the hardest to assemble in their group, so do not count on thirty minutes. Its adjustable brush speed and lowering deck were described for that bigger model, so I won't promise them here. The listing names no warranty.

Its place in the lineup is the heavy-debris pick. Buy it for acorns and pine cones. Skip it if your lawn is mostly fine clippings.

##P2
Number two, the Agri-Fab 42-Inch Tow-Behind Lawn Sweeper with 12 Cu. Ft. Hopper Bag and Easy Dump Rope.

This is for a lawn with trees, footpaths and garden beds, where a smaller sweeper threads between obstacles.

Bob Vila tested the 45-0320 and put it in its Also Consider slot. The testers said it navigates trees, footpaths and close plantings easily, and their mower did not struggle with a load of wet clippings. They rated its brush-to-wheel ratio five point one to one.

Remember the myth from number four. This is the twelve cubic foot bag that towed wet clippings without a struggle.

The listing gives a forty-two inch sweep, and brush height lets the bristles reach as deep as a half inch into the grass. You empty it with a dump rope from the seat. The wheels are eleven by two and a half inch semi-pneumatic, and the alloy steel frame weighs sixty-eight pounds. Agri-Fab lists a three-year limited warranty here, the only warranty on any listing in this video.

Now, the honest limits. The small hopper means more stops to empty, and the testers said it left more acorns and heavier debris behind than the others.

So why isn't it number one? Because on a big, dry lawn those extra stops add up, and the bigger bag picks up more per trip.

Buy it for a wooded lawn. Skip it if you have acres of open grass.

##P1
Number one, the Agri-Fab 44-Inch Tow-Behind Lawn Sweeper with 28 Cu. Ft. Flow-Through Hopper Bag.

This is for most people with a big, open lawn and a riding mower.

Bob Vila named the 45-0492 version its Best Overall pick, scoring its performance five out of five. An earlier review of an Agri-Fab 44 said it picked up nearly everything in its path and called it excellent for medium to large lawns. The exceptions were small, heavy items pressed deep into mower tracks.

The listing gives a twenty-eight cubic foot flow-through bag that fills from back to front. The testers' table says twenty-five, so treat bag size as approximate. It claims the best ratio here, five point six to one, against the Brinly's five and the Ohio Steel's four point five. You also get adjustable brush height and hitch position, a dump-from-seat handle, bronze brush shaft bushings and twelve by three inch tires. The testers offset the tongue to sweep and mow at once.

Now, the honest limits. At half full of wet clippings, the hopper was too heavy for a twenty-two horsepower mower to tow uphill. At ninety-two pounds it is the heaviest here, and the earlier review found no clutch to disengage the brush.

The listing names no warranty, so ask before you buy.

Buy it for big, dry lawns. Skip it if your hills are steep and wet.

That's why it's number one.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every sweeper on this list. The enemy is not your mower, it is the weather.

Sweep when the lawn is dry. Bob Vila's testers found a large hopper handled dry, bulky leaves well, while wet clippings made the same bag too heavy at half full. So give a rained-on lawn time to dry.

If you must sweep wet, dump at half full, not when the bag looks full.

Then set the brush height so the bristles just reach the grass. Agri-Fab's listing says its brushes go as deep as a half inch.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether your mower pulls a full load or stalls on the hill.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: how steep and how wet is your lawn? Dry and flat can take a big bag, but a slope with wet clippings needs a smaller one. No cubic foot figure on a product page answers that.

All five links are in the description. Check the listing for a warranty, a hitch that matches your mower, and the model number, because outlets often tested a sibling. Tested favorites like the Ohio Steel fifty inch are not here, because I could not find a listing to link.

Quick recap. A basic riding mower, the Brinly 42 inch. The same machine with a model number, the Brinly LS2-42BH-S. Acorns and pine cones, the Ohio Steel 4222V2. Trees and wet clippings, the Agri-Fab 42 inch. And for most big lawns, the Agri-Fab 44 inch.
