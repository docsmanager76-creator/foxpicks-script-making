##FREEUP
Now the part nobody mentions, and it costs nothing.

Do not pour anything down there first. Not before you auger.

Roy Barnes again, on why cables fail. The chemicals weaken the snake cables, so eventually they are working on a job and the cable just snaps.

Think about where that leaves you. A bowl full of caustic liquid. A tool you are cranking by hand, a foot from your face. And a cable that is now weaker than it looks.

If someone already poured, bail the bowl out and rinse it before the cable goes in. Gloves on. Then start.

That costs nothing, and it is the difference between a tool that lasts twenty years and one that snaps in the trap.
