##HOOK
It is six in the morning. The shop is thirty eight degrees. You stand at the bench, your hands go numb, and the heavy canvas coat hangs open. Sound familiar? Here is the problem. Cold in a shop is not cold on a roof. When you move all day, a light jacket works. When you stand still, you need real lining and a hood. Here is what I found. WERD did partly hands on testing, and Pro Tool Reviews tested heated jackets, but no outlet tested all of these. I will show you which jackets keep a still body warm, which suit a moving crew, and which one heats itself. One famous name is the villain, and it has a hood problem. And you get a free one minute fit check. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the Dickies TJ15 men's insulated Eisenhower front zip work jacket.

This is for a crew that moves all day in mild cold and wants a light jacket without a hood.

The shell is poly cotton twill, sixty five percent polyester and thirty five percent cotton. The listing says it is wrinkle resistant and vat dyed, to hold its color. The lining is quilted nylon taffeta, and the whole jacket weighs one point six pounds. Dickies calls it both water repellent and water resistant. You get two slash front welt pockets, adjustable tabs at the waist, and a heavy duty brass zipper. There is a pencil pocket on the left sleeve. The fit is relaxed, with a point collar and no hood. Remember the hood. It comes back at number five. Care is machine wash. The listing sends you to the maker's site for warranty terms, and I found no term in years. North American Outdoorsman compiled specs, did not test, and recommended this jacket as a lower cost choice.

Now, the honest limits. Nobody tested this jacket. Dickies calls it lightweight but warm, and that means mild cold, not a still shop in January. There is no hood, and no fleece in the body.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick.

Buy it if you move all day and the cold is mild. Skip it if you stand still at a bench.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The Carhartt 103828 men's Iconic J01 duck Detroit jacket, the famous name.

On paper it looks great. It is twelve ounce, one hundred percent cotton duck. The body has a blanket lining, and the sleeves have quilted nylon.

Now the catch. Remember the hood? This jacket has none. It has a corduroy trimmed collar with snaps, made for an optional hood. The listing also gives no insulation grams. A thick shell is not a warm jacket, because the lining and the hood do the work. Number three has a thinner nylon shell, yet it carries one hundred fifty grams and an insulated hood. No outlet measured warmth here, so this comes from reading specs. I am not calling it bad. But for a cold shop, a famous name does not fix a missing hood.

Want a hood and a warm body? Number three. Want one coat for most jobs? Number one.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it takes one minute.

First, wear your real base layer and a hoodie when you try the jacket on.

Second, zip up and reach forward like you hold a tool. If the back pulls tight, size up.

Third, raise your arms. The cuffs and the waist should not ride up.

Fourth, match the jacket to the job. Remember the hook? Moving all day wants a light coat. Standing still wants lining and a hood.

Fifth, sit down in it. It should not bunch at the belly.

Sixth, fit the heated one over the base layer, as Pro Tool Reviews advises. And read its care tag before you wash it.

That costs nothing, and it takes one minute.
