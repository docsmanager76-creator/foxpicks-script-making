##HOOK
Impact rated sounds like a bit that can take anything. Pro Tool Reviews tested that. Its testers drove three-inch exterior screws into hard laminated veneer lumber with two brands of bits. One brand averaged about twenty-five screws before the bit snapped. The other managed about twelve before its head warped and stripped. Same screws, same wood, and a gap of more than two to one that nothing on either package could have told you about.

Heavy driving is where that gap shows up, in lag screws, deck screws and framing, where a bit that fails costs you the screw, the time and the board. Six impact driver bit sets made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every set here. It costs nothing, and it can double the life of a bit.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not from a lab test.

First, use impact-rated bits only. Standard bits are not built for impact drivers, and the makers say so.

Second, match the bit to the screw. A bit that fits snugly cams out less, and a loose fit rounds the tip.

Third, push straight and press firmly. Bits slip and strip when they are tilted.

Fourth, use the shortest bit that reaches. A long bit flexes more, so keep extensions for when you need them.

Fifth, replace a bit when its tip rounds. A worn tip strips screws and breaks sooner.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this number come from a test or from a label? Fifty times the life and a hundred times the life are labels. About twenty-five screws and about twelve screws came from testers who counted them.

All seven links are in the description, including the Milwaukee. Check the piece count, check which tip types you need, check the bit length, check whether nut drivers are included, and check the case before you buy.
