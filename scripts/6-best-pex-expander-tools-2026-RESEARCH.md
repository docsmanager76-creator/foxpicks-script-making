## 2. Buyer-decision checklist
1. Pipe type: expansion tools work on PEX-A only. They cannot make a PEX-B joint (callback to the crimp video).
2. Fitting system: ASTM F1960 expansion fittings (ProPEX, PowerPEX, ExpansionPEX) need a F1960 expander; crimp rings and cinch clamps are a different system.
3. Sizes: 1/2, 3/4, 1 in covers most homes; 3/8 head optional; 1-1/2 in needs a larger tool.
4. Manual vs powered: manual = ratchet handles, auto-rotate head, lots of squeezing; powered = trigger, battery platform.
5. Battery platform and what is sold separately (tool only vs kit).
6. Heads: brand and size; compatibility claims are maker claims, not fit tests.
7. Grease on the cone every time (PTR, ToolRev); without it the head can seize or not rotate.
8. Tight spaces: slim body, paddle trigger, tool height.
9. Cold weather: cold PEX is harder to expand and slower (not tested by us; PTR snippet only, UNVERIFIED).
10. Warranty, in the box (cutter, removal tool, case).

## 5. Hook (type 5, Absurd Number) — raw material
After the tool opens the pipe, PEX-A shrinks back and you have about five to seven seconds to seat the fitting (ToolRev, warm shop, 3/4 in; one tester). Manuals suggest eleven to twelve pumps for 3/4 in, the tester found six enough in warm weather. Hook: "You have five seconds. Then the pipe closes." Promise: which tool opens it fast enough, the famous name that is the villain, and a one-minute free check (grease, size, window). Close with "All the product links are in the description."

## 6. Myth (spec-sheet leader loses a measured test)
Milwaukee's M12 is the spec leader on speed claims; ToolRev found it slightly faster on pumping but the DeWalt opened the pipe about 3/32 in wider and PTR timed the DeWalt first (2:09.54 vs 2:11.17). Told at #1/#2 as "the faster name on the box is not the faster tool in the test", but both outlets call the gap tiny. Do not overstate.

## 7. Villain (type C, famous name): Milwaukee 2532-20
Why: the name every plumber knows; on Amazon the listing has no bullets, no past-month sales signal, and tool-only is implied by "-20" (UNVERIFIED on the listing); ToolRev found noticeable play in all three Milwaukee 2532 units and the work light shut off after 11 seconds vs 2.5 minutes. Fair statement: not a bad tool, an M12 owner's tool; if you do not own M12 batteries it is the wrong buy. Price never spoken. Redirect forward to #3 (powered, batteries included) and #2.
Alternative villain if you prefer: Milwaukee 2474-22 or DeWalt DCE410 (1-1/2 in, 21 ratings, no badge).
