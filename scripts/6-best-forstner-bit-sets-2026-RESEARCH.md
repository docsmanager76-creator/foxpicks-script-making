## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST

Taken from Bob Vila's 2026 test rubric and buying guide, Fine Woodworking's 18-brand tool test (2008), and Woodsmith's buying criteria.

1. **Hole quality**: how smooth the walls and bottom are, and tearout at the rim (Bob Vila "Hole Quality"; FWW rim quality)
2. **Cutting edge type**: twin-tooth or standard, sawtooth, serrated or wave (decides smoothness against speed)
3. **Steel**: carbon steel (HCS), HSS, alloy or chrome steel, or carbide-tipped (decides heat tolerance, edge life, motor load)
4. **Real size count vs piece count**: duplicate bits, and the largest diameter (most sets top out at 2 or 2-1/8 inches)
5. **Shank**: round or hex, 3/8 or 1/4 inch, reduced shank (decides chuck fit and slipping)
6. **Overlapping and angled holes**: rim and spur design (FWW: bits with the largest rim did best)
7. **Speed and motor load**: Bob Vila measured drilling speed and noted bogging; Fisch claims 25% less power
8. **Chip clearing and clogging**: Bob Vila, FWW
9. **Edge retention and whether it can be sharpened**: Bob Vila (no bench grinder; carbide needs diamond)
10. **Drill press vs handheld**: large bits need a press (the ASNOMY listing warns about this itself)
11. **Case**: wood, blow-molded, or none
12. **Warranty**

## 5. HOOK — type **6 Category Lie** (last script used 5; allowed)

"14-piece" on the box is not 14 sizes. Two of the tested 14-piece sets list the **1/4-inch bit twice** in their own size lists, so 14 pieces means 13 holes. A 16-piece set in 1/8-inch steps really is 16. Bob Vila: bit count matters less than quality for pro use. Name Bob Vila (8 kits, oak, pine, plywood, overlapping and angled holes); no pick brands in the hook.
Backup: **4 Contradiction**: same Bob Vila test, the set that drilled *faster than average* left the *roughest walls* (WEN), and the carbide set that *stayed sharp* *bogged down* both drills.

## 6. MYTH — "carbide means better"

- Bob Vila: the Hercules carbide set stayed sharp, but **both the hand drill and the drill press bogged down**, and one bit **chipped after a drop on concrete**.
- FWW 2008: Freud's carbide Forstners showed **no clear edge over steel** in their limited 100-hole hickory endurance run (secondary summary; PDF unreadable, so it is a *soft* source and gets one line at most).
- Bob Vila's own guide: carbide lasts up to 10× longer in hardwood and needs a diamond sharpener.
- Line: *"You are buying an edge shape, not a metal."* Planted at #4 (the carbide pick, which needs a drill press), paid at #1/#2 (steel sets that won on hole quality).

## 9. FREE UPGRADE

Speed and chips, not pressure: run big bits slow, clear chips by backing out every half inch or so, and clamp the work. Bob Vila: Bosch steel "needs steady pressure and moderate speed", overheating shortens edge life, and you can't sharpen Forstners on a bench grinder. FWW: cleaner-cutting bits ran cooler. Exact RPM-by-diameter chart: **need a maker chart** (Freud or Fisch publish one; to be fetched at the script stage, or keep it qualitative).
