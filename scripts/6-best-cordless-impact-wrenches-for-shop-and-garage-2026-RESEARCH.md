## 3. Buyer checklist (shop and garage)
1. Drive size: 3/8" (tight spaces) vs 1/2" (lugs, suspension).
2. Fastening torque vs breakaway ("nut-busting") torque — different numbers; makers' boxes disagree with tests.
3. Control: speed/torque modes, auto shut-off, bolt-removal mode.
4. Size and weight (tool only), reach into a bay.
5. Anvil: hog/friction ring vs detent pin.
6. Battery platform you already own; bare tool vs kit.
7. Warranty term.
8. Light, dust/oil resistance.

## 6. Myth
"The biggest torque number wins." Boxes quote fastening, breakaway, or reverse; none is comparable across brands, and the lug nut spec on most cars is far below any of these. (Lug-nut spec is the owner's manual value — UNVERIFIED for this video beyond the earlier dossier's Civic 80 ft-lb note.)

## 9. Free upgrade (≈128–135 words)
Read which number is on the box (fastening, breakaway, reverse); check the owner's manual torque spec for your lug nuts; use impact-rated sockets; set the lowest mode first then step up; torque-wrench the final turn on wheels; keep a spare battery charged. No purchase needed.
