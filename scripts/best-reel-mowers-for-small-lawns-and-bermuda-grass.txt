##HOOK
Every reel mower listing promises a clean, even cut. Bermuda grass asks for one more thing. It wants that cut low. The University of Florida puts Bermuda at half an inch to an inch and a half. Of the six mowers here, only one names Bermuda in its own listing. Three of them stop at about an inch. And Bob Vila pushed reel mowers across real Bermuda in Southern yards, then named a best overall that stops at one inch and weighs fifty two pounds. That mower is the villain. Here is what is coming. The lightest pick, the widest one, and the one built for Bermuda. Why more blades is not always better. And a free habit that makes any reel cut cleaner. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the American Lawn Mower 1204-14 14-Inch 4-Blade Push Reel Lawn Mower.

This is for a tiny front yard or a side strip, and for anyone pushing a reel mower for the first time.

It is the lightest mower in this lineup. The maker says nineteen pounds, so you can lift it over a step with one hand. The cut is fourteen inches wide, from four alloy steel blades. It rolls on eight and a half inch polymer wheels with a plain T-handle. The maker lists it for lawns from five hundred to three thousand square feet. The cutting height runs from half an inch to an inch and three quarters. Hold that number. Half an inch is the bottom of the Bermuda range, and three mowers above this one cannot reach it. The maker also says it handles grass up to four inches tall, and gets less effective past that.

Those Southern testers found it the lightest and easiest to push of everything they tried, with a folding handle and a sturdy feel.

Now, the honest limits. In dense or overgrown grass it often needed a second pass. And changing the height takes a wrench, not a lever.

There is nothing to fuel, charge, or tune. Its place in the lineup is the entry pick.

Buy it for a small lawn you keep short. Skip it if you let the grass get long.

##P5
Number five, the Great States 415-16 16-Inch 5-Blade Push Reel Lawn Mower.

This is for a normal small lawn, where you want a wider cut than number six without moving up a tier.

Bob Vila named it the best bang for the buck. Their testers praised a smooth cut, a cushioned T-handle, and rear rollers. The cut is sixteen inches wide, two more than number six, so you walk fewer rows. The five blades spin on a ball bearing reel, in heat treated alloy steel that the maker says stays sharp longer. It rolls on ten inch composite wheels, and it goes together without tools.

Now the myth. Most buyers think more blades always means a better cut. The same testers found the seven blade mower in this lineup cut the cleanest, but it was harder to push. And this five blade mower moved through dense grass more easily than the wider models. Blade count buys finish, not ease. You are choosing a trade, not a winner.

Now, the honest limits. The cutting height runs from one inch to two and a half. One inch works for common Bermuda. But hybrid Bermuda, kept golf short, wants half an inch, and this mower cannot go there.

The listing gives a one year limited warranty. Its place in the lineup is the value pick.

Buy it for a common Bermuda or zoysia lawn. Skip it for a hybrid kept very short.

##P4
Number four, the LawnMaster LMRM1801 18-Inch 5-Blade Push Reel Lawn Mower with Grass Bag.

This is for a small lawn where you hate leaving clippings on the walkway.

It is the only mower in this lineup with a collection bag in the box. The bag holds thirty five liters. The cut is eighteen inches wide, which is four inches more than number six. The five blades are hardened steel, and LawnMaster calls the action scissor like, with no ripping or tearing. The front wheels are ten inches, for easy turns around beds and trees. The height has nine positions, from about one point one inches to two and a half. The maker designed it for small lawns, and it runs with no gas and no battery.

There is a quieter reason it is here. Popular Mechanics notes that Great States and Scotts share one parent company. Most reel mowers on this list come from that one family. LawnMaster is the different maker here, so you get a second design to compare.

Now, the honest limits. No outlet I found tested this mower. Everything here comes from its own listing. And at a little over an inch, it has the highest floor in this lineup.

Its place in the lineup is the bagging pick.

Buy it if you want clippings caught. Skip it for hybrid Bermuda, or if you want a tested mower.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The Fiskars StaySharp Max 18-Inch Reel Mower.

It is the critics' favorite. Bob Vila named it the best overall reel mower. BestReviews put its seventeen inch version first. LawnStarter named it best for large yards.

On paper, it earns that. It has hardened steel blades, a single lever height adjustment, and a cut that reaches within an inch of an edge. It was the only manual reel in that test that mowed through sticks.

Now the catch. It weighs fifty two pounds, and its lowest cut is one inch. Their own earlier review warned it may struggle with dense grass at a low height. Picture pushing that weight across thick summer Bermuda. A Bermuda mower that cannot cut low is a fescue mower with extra steps.

Want low and light? Number six. Want low and wide? Number two.

##P3
Number three, the Scotts 2000-20S 20-Inch 5-Blade Push Reel Lawn Mower.

This is for the bigger end of a small lawn, where you want fewer passes on a Saturday.

It has the widest cut in this lineup, twenty inches. That is six inches more than number six, so a lawn takes noticeably fewer rows. The same testers named it best for large yards, for lawns up to about a quarter acre. They liked its raised loop handle with several grip positions, and found it jammed less than most of the reels they ran. The five blades sit on a ball bearing reel, in heat treated alloy steel. It rolls on ten inch dual tracking wheels. The height adjusts with a quick snap, from one inch to three. Assembly needs no tools, and it runs with no gas or oil. The listing names the American Lawn Mower Company as its maker.

Now, the honest limits. The same testers found its gear drive can jam on sticks and debris, so walk the lawn first. And remember the half inch at number six? This one stops at one inch. Hold that, because number two fixes it.

There is nothing to fuel or charge, and no belts to buy. Its place in the lineup is the large pick.

Buy it for common Bermuda or a wider lawn. Skip it for hybrid Bermuda, or a yard full of twigs.

##P2
Number two, the Great States 815-18 18-Inch 5-Blade Push Reel Lawn Mower.

This is for a bigger small lawn that still needs a true Bermuda cut.

Remember the one inch floor at number three? This one goes down to half an inch, and up to two and three quarters. That is the widest height range in this lineup, so it covers a hybrid kept short and a common lawn left taller. The cut is eighteen inches, only two less than number three. The five blades ride a ball bearing reel, in alloy steel the maker says stays sharp longer. It rolls on ten inch composite wheels, with a loop handle and cushioned grips, and assembles without tools. It weighs twenty seven pounds. The listing gives a two year limited warranty, twice the term of number five.

The Southern test covered its eighteen inch sibling from the American Lawn Mower Company and named it the most durable. The testers praised the all steel build, the broad height range, and two rear rollers.

So why isn't it number one? That test was the sibling, not this exact listing. The sibling's height change took two bolts. And this mower has five blades, not seven, and its listing never names Bermuda.

Its place in the lineup is the premium capability pick.

Buy it if your lawn is too big for sixteen inches. Skip it if you want the lightest push.

Its place in the lineup is the best for most pick.

Buy it for a Bermuda lawn you mow often. Skip it for overgrown grass.

The enemy of a reel mower is not the grass. It is a dull edge. Toro, which builds reel mowers for golf courses, says rounded edges on the reel and the bedknife pinch and tear the grass instead of shearing it. That is a ragged cut, from a mower bought for a clean one.

So keep the edges sharp, and keep the work light. The maker of number six says to cut no more than a third of the blade at a time, and never when the grass is wet. In peak summer, Bermuda grows fast, so mow more often, not lower. Short, frequent passes are also easier to push.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether your reel cuts or chews.
