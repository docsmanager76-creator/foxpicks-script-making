## 5. Hook — type 6, Category Lie
- Every box leads with CFM. Same 30-inch size: Amazon Basics 12,480, VEVOR 13,000. Same Tornado 24: listing says 7,700, Bob Vila's table says 8,540 on high. Car and Driver, which measured airspeed with an anemometer, would not publish CFM because accurate CFM needs a lab.
- Payoff: Bob Vila scored the Tornado (8,540 CFM) and the Maxx Air (4,000 CFM) the same 4.5 on air. Half the CFM, same score.
- Open loops: (1) which fan wins on air, (2) the myth (CFM), (3) the Upgrade Pick I would skip (villain), (4) the free habit (placement).
- Backup hook: **3 Reader's Pain** (hot garage, 90F, a fan that just moves hot air).

## 6. Myth — "bigger CFM number = more cooling"
- Bob Vila air scores: Tornado 8,540 CFM = 4.5; Maxx Air 4,000 CFM = 4.5; Air King 3,670 CFM = 4.3.
- The same 24-inch Tornado has two CFM numbers in two places. Same-size 30 in fans claim 12,480 and 13,000.
- No outlet measured CFM. Say "claimed" or "rated".

## 9. Free upgrade — placement
- Hunter (a fan maker, so attributed): mount or aim high toward the open garage door to push hot air out and pull cooler air in. Garage Journal posters (anecdotal): you need an opening for air to enter; a fan alone does not cool the room, it moves air across you.
- Safe script version: open two openings, aim the fan out the door, stand in the airflow, and do not expect air temperature to drop. Do not claim degrees.
