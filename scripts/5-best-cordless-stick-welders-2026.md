##FREE
One habit costs nothing, and it's the difference between finishing a job and walking back to the truck for a battery that isn't there.

Count your rods, not your minutes. Every cordless stick welder in this video that publishes a runtime spec does it in electrode count, not vague minutes. Lincoln states up to twenty-one electrodes on a charge. ESAB states up to thirty-three E6013 electrodes on its largest battery kit. Those are real, manufacturer-published numbers, not a guess.

Before you head to a job with no outlet in sight, count how many joints you actually need to run. Match that against the electrode count your specific welder publishes for the rod you're using. It takes thirty seconds, and it's a real number you can plan around instead of a battery percentage that tells you nothing about how many welds are actually left in it.
