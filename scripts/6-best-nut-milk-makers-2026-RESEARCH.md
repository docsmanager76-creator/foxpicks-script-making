## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST (from Wired, Reviewed, Engadget, Homes & Gardens, Esquire, BestReviews criteria)

2. Milk quality: grit / smoothness, pulp dryness
3. Prep: soak needed? strain needed?
4. Batch size per cycle (cups) and cycle time
5. Heating: can it cook soy / make hot milk?
6. Cleaning: auto-clean, dishwasher-safe parts, filter-basket mess
7. Noise (dB) and counter footprint / weight
8. What is in the box (pitcher, brush, bag, recipes)
9. Warranty and replacement-part availability
10. Ingredient range: oats, coconut, soy, nuts

## HOOK — type 4 Controlled Contradiction (rotation: last script hook 5 / villain D, so hook 5 and villain D are out)
Raw material: Wired Nov 2025 names the ChefWave "best for families and large batches". Its own listing says 10 or 20 oz per batch, and Wired measures about 2.5 cups. In the same Wired article the "best for almond milk" machine makes 5 cups, and Engadget clocked that machine at about 90 seconds of blending while Reviewed's ChefWave took 12 to 16 minutes for 20 oz. "Large batch" label vs measured batch. No pick brands in hook; Wired, Reviewed, Engadget named.
Alternative if you prefer: hook 6 Category Lie ("no soak, no strain" printed on nearly every listing, yet Reviewed tasted grit and Wired found clogging).

## MYTH (L2) — UNVERIFIED as a measured spec-vs-measured pair
"No soaking, no straining" on the box does not mean smooth. ChefWave listing: "pulverizes and filters automatically, no nut milk bag needed". Reviewed: grainier than store-bought, "only if you're okay straining". Filter-basket machines (Almond Cow, Nama) strain by design. I have no two-number measured test; this is a claim-vs-tester contradiction, so I'd phrase it that way, not as numbers. Tell me if you want a harder myth.

## FREE UPGRADE
Soak almonds overnight (Reviewed 2020: soaked almonds gave creamier, less bitter milk than unsoaked) and make small batches: homemade milk lasts only 3 to 5 days (Engadget); keep the pulp for recipes. H&G adds: coconut alone is watery, soak it with dates and cashews. Costs nothing, works on every machine.
