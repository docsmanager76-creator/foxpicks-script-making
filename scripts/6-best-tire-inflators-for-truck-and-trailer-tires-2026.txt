##HOOK
Four inflators in one truck tire test were rated one hundred fifty psi. Not one of them reached fifty psi. A unit rated just ninety-nine did. That gap between the number on the box and the number on the tire is why most inflator lists mislead truck owners. Here is the part nobody tells you. Only one outlet, Outdoor Life, tested inflators on real truck tires, and nobody has tested them on trailer tires. So I used that test, the passenger tire tests, and the maker specs, and I will label which is which. You will also meet an inflator that two outlets praised, and it cannot do the job a trailer needs. And you will get a free habit that stops you trusting the wrong gauge. All the product links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it matters most on trailers.

Check pressure when the tires are cold, and fill to the number on the vehicle placard or the trailer sticker, not the sidewall. The sidewall number is the tire's maximum, not your target. That is standard tire guidance, so confirm it on your own placard.

Then check your inflator's gauge against a separate gauge. Reviewed found some gauges off by five psi, and one by fifteen. Car and Driver found the Viair 88P's gauge about ten psi high.

On a load range E trailer tire, that error matters. A tire at seventy might read eighty.

Fill, then double-check with the other gauge. Do it before a trip, not at the roadside.

That costs nothing, and it takes a minute.
