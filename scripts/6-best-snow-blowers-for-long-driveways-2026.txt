##HOOK
One listing says its snow blower moves twenty-five hundred pounds of snow a minute and weighs one hundred eighty-seven pounds. Another claims nineteen hundred and weighs three hundred fifty-eight. If bigger numbers meant a cleaner driveway, the first would win, and nobody who has used both would say so.

So I went to people who did the work. Bob Vila's team ran over thirty-five models through ten inches of heavy wet snow. Consumer Reports lab results, as a TV station reported them, cover speed and throw. Six machines made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why.

Before that, one battery family where two outlets disagree. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here are the free habits, and they work with every machine here.

First, don't overload it. Bob Vila saw one electric model stall when it was loaded too quickly. Slow your walking speed, take a narrower bite into a plow pile, and let the auger catch up.

Second, plan around the batteries. Bob Vila says the EGO twenty-four inch batteries get hot and take hours to recharge. If you own an electric, charge both batteries before the storm and check them before the second round.

Third, set the skid shoes for your surface. A PowerSmart listing says its skid shoes adjust for paved and gravel driveways, and a machine scraping gravel throws gravel. Set them before you start, not after.

All three cost nothing, and they matter more than any pounds-a-minute number.
