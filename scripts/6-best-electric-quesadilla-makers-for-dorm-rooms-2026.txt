##HOOK
A dorm room gives you one outlet, one desk and one roommate who wants the lights off. It also gives you a housing inspector. University Parent reports that inspectors look for an exposed heating element, and for anything that could be left running unattended. Many schools allow heating appliances only with a concealed element and automatic shut-off, and some ban them completely. Rules vary by school, so I cannot tell you which of these yours allows, and nobody can until you read your own housing list. A cheap appliance is no bargain if it gets confiscated at move-in.

What I can tell you is what each machine actually offers. Six electric quesadilla makers made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every machine here.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not from a lab test.

First, check your school's prohibited-appliance list before you buy. Rules vary.

Second, unplug it when you are done. These listings mention no on and off switch.

Third, never leave it running unattended, which is the inspector's second question from the hook.

Fourth, use a flat tortilla and a moderate filling, so the latch closes properly.

Fifth, wipe the plates with a damp cloth once they cool. The maker says no abrasive cleaners, so skip the steel wool. Sixth, let it cool before you stand it upright. Storing a hot plate on a shelf is how scorch marks happen.
