##HOOK
A handheld vacuum is a handheld vacuum. That is what the box says. Then testers measured them. Bob Vila's team ran eight models through six tests, with flour, oatmeal, rice, cereal, pet hair and dirt. One measured fifteen point two air watts. Another measured thirty-five. Same category, more than double the suction, and you cannot see that on the box.

Runtime told the same story. Vacuum Wars tested twenty-plus cordless handhelds, and the average ran about eleven minutes at full power. One model in Bob Vila's test ran about thirty.

So the label tells you almost nothing about what you are buying. Six handheld vacuums made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the Black+Decker Dustbuster HHVI315JO42 Cordless Handheld Vacuum.

This is for someone who wants a light, simple vacuum for crumbs, car seats and stairs, and does not want to spend much on it.

The listing gives a weight of two point one pounds, a wide-mouth nozzle for scooping up big messes, and a crevice tool for narrow spaces. The dirt bowl is see-through, so you can tell at a glance when it needs emptying, and both the bowl and the filters wash off. The listing says about fifteen minutes of runtime and four hours or so to charge. It runs at seventy-five decibels, the filter is cloth, and a jack-plug charger comes in the box. Black+Decker lists a two-year limited warranty.

Vacuum Wars named it the Budget Pick in a test of twenty-plus handhelds, which measured suction, airflow, battery life, noise and debris pickup. Its testers liked how light it was and how well the wide mouth worked.

Now, the honest limits. Vacuum Wars found the battery short, the filtration basic, and no motorized brush for embedded hair. Remember the hook: a plain wide-mouth nozzle is fine on crumbs, and it is not built for hair ground into carpet.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick.

Buy it if you want a light vacuum for everyday spills. Do not buy it if you have pets that shed.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every vacuum here.

These tips come from the testers' notes and the maintenance lines in the listings, not from a lab test.

First, empty the bin after every use. A full bin weakens suction, and the bins on these are small, so it fills fast.

Second, wash the filter if the listing says it washes. Several of these do, and the testers found filtration was a weak point on the cheaper ones. Let it dry completely.

Third, use the motorized brush for hair and the plain nozzle for crumbs. Bob Vila's testers found hair came up best with the brush.

Fourth, charge it on its dock when you finish, so it is ready. Vacuum Wars measured about five hours for an average recharge.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this number come from a test or from a label? Ten minutes, seventeen minutes, twenty minutes are claims. Fifteen point two air watts, twenty-nine, and about eleven minutes on average came from testers who measured them.

All seven links are in the description, including the Shark. Check the weight, check the bin size, check the warranty, and check whether the brush matches your mess before you buy.

Quick recap. The Black+Decker HHVI315JO42 is the entry pick. The CHV1410L is the value pick. The Shark WANDVAC is the lightweight specialist. The Furbuster is the power pick. The Pivot Vac is the pivot-nozzle pick. And for most people, the Bissell Pet Hair Eraser.

Prices move fast, so check before you buy. One thing: if you own one of these, tell me how it did on pet hair. If enough of you post, we will compare.
