##HOOK
Look at the number on the box of an SDS-plus rotary hammer. One inch, say. ToolGuyd explains that this is a maximum capacity, not where the tool works best. For a one-inch hammer, the optimal hole is about half an inch, and the range runs from roughly five-sixteenths to five-eighths. Now look at the garage. People mounting four-post lifts on Garage Journal talk about three-quarter-inch anchors, with two anchors per post. So the number on the box and the hole you actually need are two different numbers. And I found no outlet that tested these hammers for automotive work, so this list leans on tests of the tools and on their specs. Six cordless rotary hammers made the list, I will warn you off one more, and number one comes last. All the links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any hammer on this list.

These tips come from maker guidance and common practice, not a lab test.

First, read your lift maker's instructions for anchor size and floor thickness. Garage Journal says some advise against drilling all the way through.

Second, match the bit to the anchor maker's table.

Third, use hammer mode and set the depth rod so every hole is the right depth.

Fourth, keep the side handle on.

Fifth, clear the dust from the hole before you set the anchor.

Sixth, use dust extraction where the listing offers it, and wear a dust mask. Concrete dust is a hazard, and that is why several of these listings mention OSHA Table 1.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this claim come from a test or from a label? One inch is a label. Two point seven one joules and class-leading came from Bob Vila's test. The sweet spot of a hammer is not on the box, either.

All seven links are in the description, including the FLEX. Check the hole size you need, the impact energy, the weight, and whether you already own the battery. Then read your anchor maker's instructions before you drill.

Here is the quick recap. The Makita is the lightweight pick. The Bosch is the kickback-control pick. The DeWalt five-eighths is the small-hole pick. The DeWalt one-and-an-eighth is the power pick. The Milwaukee is the safety pick. And for most people, the DeWalt DCH273B.
