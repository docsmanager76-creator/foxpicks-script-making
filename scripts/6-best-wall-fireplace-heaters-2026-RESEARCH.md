## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST
1. Mount type: flat on the wall, recessed into the studs, or both (and whether the frame fits 2x4 or 2x6 framing)
2. Depth and recess opening size
3. Heater: watts (750/1500), BTU, rated area, and whether the maker calls it supplemental
4. Thermostat: built in, with its range, or on/off only
5. Amps, cord vs hardwire, and circuit advice
6. Clearance above for a TV or mantel, and where the heat vent sits
7. Flame: colors, speeds, brightness, and media (logs, crystals, driftwood); flame with no heat
8. Controls: remote, touch panel, timer, app or voice
9. Noise (claimed dB)
10. Weight and how many people it takes to hang
11. Ownership: warranty, what's in the box (bracket, media), certification (ETL/CETL)

## HOOK — type 1 COLLISION
Two wall fireplaces sit on the same aisle. One runs **1,500 watts** and says it heats **up to 400 square feet**. The other runs **1,400 watts**, less power, and says **up to 1,000 square feet** (Dimplex Sierra 48 listing; the brand is not named in the hook). The best-selling recessed model's own maker says its heater will "take the edge off a cool room" (Touchstone listing). This Old House relays owners calling heaters like it nearly ineffective. Named outlets: Bob Vila and This Old House. No pick brands. Promises: which number to trust; the "best overall" that cannot go on a wall; the clearance number that decides where your TV goes.

## MYTH — a wider fireplace is a bigger heater
The 68" R.W.Flame, the 36" Antarctic Star, the 60" Bernice and the reserves (Electactic 36, EUHOMY 50, Tehanld 60) all list **1,500 W, about 5,100 BTU, 400 sq ft**. Width buys glass and flame, not heat. Line: *"You are buying a screen, not a bigger heater."* Planted at #5 (36 in, same 400 as the 68), paid at #3.

## FREE UPGRADE — read the clearance number before you mount the TV
The makers disagree on clearance: Touchstone says 8 in, Westinghouse says 11.8 in, and Bernice puts its vents out of sight so a TV can go above it. Measure from the top of the fireplace to the **bottom edge of the TV**, not to the bracket. Check where the heat vent blows, top front or hidden. Plug directly into a wall outlet, never an extension cord or power strip (NFPA space-heater guidance; Touchstone's own 12 A and "20 A may be necessary" note). It costs nothing and decides the whole wall layout.
