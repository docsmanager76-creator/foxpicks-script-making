## STEP 0: BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST (beginner lens)

Built from the TechGearLab rubric (Heating 50%, Ergonomics, Ease of Use), the Testix test method (temp settings, weight, runtime, paint area per charge, dB), and the EPA RRP lead rule.

1. **Temperature control**: one fixed heat, two settings, or a variable dial or LCD. Beginners need a LOW setting for vinyl, shrink tube and PVC.
2. **Low and high temps in °F**: the low end matters as much as the max
3. **Airflow (CFM)**: more air spreads the heat. One setting or two
4. **Runtime per charge, and which battery it was quoted on**: makers quote 4 Ah or 6 Ah packs
5. **Time to operating temp**
6. **Bare weight**
7. **Lock-on for hands-free use** and lock-off trigger safety
8. **Nozzles included**: flat or spreader, hook or reflector, concentrator. Can corded nozzles fit?
9. **Battery platform**: do you already own one? Is a battery included?
10. **Safety**: overheat cut-off, cool-down, a stand to sit it upright
11. **Light**: LED for dark cabinets and dashboards
12. **Warranty**

## 4. OPEN DECISIONS (please answer)

**D1: Milwaukee as villain (recommended).** The 2688-20 is Bob Vila's "Best Cordless" and passes every Amazon rule, but for a *beginner* it is the wrong first gun. It has one fixed 875°F heat, no low setting, no lock-on and no nozzles in the box. TechGearLab gave it the lowest heating score of the cordless guns (6.4) and said the safety sits "strangely high". Using it as the villain costs a pick, so #4 becomes the off-brand LIVOWALNY (untested, borderline 4.0).

**D2: #6 = WORKPRO or SEEKONE HG450?** WORKPRO has far more history (Oct 2025, 1K+ badge). The SEEKONE adds an LCD and 212–932°F, but has 20 ratings and launched Mar 2026. **Recommended: WORKPRO.**

## 8. FREE UPGRADE: test old paint before you heat it
EPA's lead Renovation, Repair and Painting rule prohibits **operating a heat gun at or above 1,100°F** on lead paint, and homes **built before 1978** may have it. All six picks top out below 1,100°F, but even under that line, heated lead paint makes fumes. Buy a lead test swab, start on the low setting, keep the nozzle moving 1–2 inches off the surface, and let the nozzle cool on its stand. **Source: EPA RRP (40 CFR 745.85).** Confirm the exact wording before scripting.
