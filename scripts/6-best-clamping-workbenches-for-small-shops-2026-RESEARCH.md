## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST

From Bob Vila's test method and buying guide (size/weight, material, stability, clamping, extra features, adjustability). Ownership items were added on top.

1. **Clamping system**: what's in the box (bar clamps, dogs, vise jaws), clamp force, horizontal vs vertical clamping
2. **Top size**: working area in inches
3. **Working height**: fixed or adjustable
4. **Load capacity**: table vs sawhorse, and how it's stated ("evenly distributed")
5. **Weight**: carrying it in a small shop
6. **Folded size**: storage footprint, the whole point for a small shop
7. **Frame and top material**: ABS/PP resin, bamboo, steel or aluminum legs
8. **Stability**: feet, leg lock, front-to-back wobble (Bob Vila)
9. **Expandability**: linking to sister tables, accessory rails
10. **Setup**: assembly required or not
11. **Warranty**

## 4. HOOK — **Type 4 Controlled Contradiction** (ledger: last script used 3)

Raw material: in Bob Vila's hands-on test, the top pick was the **300 lb** table, ranked above two benches rated at **1,000 lb**. The reason given was that its clamps were the best in the test. The heavy-duty Keter "might've been" best overall, but its clamp guides weren't smooth. The DeWalt is also rated at 1,000 lb, ships with no clamps at all, and wobbled front to back.
Draft line: *"One test, three benches. The one rated for three hundred pounds beat two rated for a thousand. On a clamping bench, the clamp is the product."* (No pick names in the first two sentences.)

## 5. MYTH — load capacity predicts a good clamping bench

- **Spec-sheet leader that loses:** the Keter (1,000 lb) and the DeWalt (1,000 lb) both lost to the Pegasus (300 lb) in Bob Vila's test.
- **Capacity numbers aren't even consistent:** the Oncupid listing says 1,650 lb in its title and 1,000 lb in its bullet. The Pegasus and Pony state one number as a table and a different one as a sawhorse (300/1,000 and 500/1,000). Klein says 1,000 lb "evenly distributed".
- **Caveat:** Bob Vila's result is qualitative (a ranking), not a measured number. Plant the myth at #3 Keter, pay it off at #1 Pegasus.

## 6. VILLAIN — **Type C Overpriced Premium**: Rockwell JawHorse RK9003 (not a pick)

- **Steel-man:** Pro Tool Reviews and Tool Box Buzz have both reviewed it hands-on (pages blocked to me, search snippets only). Its listing claims a **2,200 lb (one ton) foot-pedal clamping force**, a **37 in jaw range** (holds a 36 in door), a **600 lb load**, all-steel construction, and a 5-year Rockwell warranty in the description (the spec table says 2-year Positec, a conflict).
- **Kill shot, from its own listing:** it weighs **46.1 lb** in the spec table (the description says 43). That's 3½ times the Sidekick, and heavier than any of the six. It has **no flat work surface**: it's a vise on a tripod, so a small shop still needs a bench beside it. Line: *"It's a world-class vise. It isn't a bench, and a small shop has room for one, not both."*
- **Weak point:** I couldn't read the PTR and TBB pages, so the "who recommends it" leg rests on titles and snippets. Fallback: name only PTR (title confirmed), or swap to the WEN tilting bench (Bob Vila: the clamp wouldn't lock). That one is type D, though, and the last script used D.

## 7. CALLBACKS

- **C1:** #6 Sidekick weighs **12.6 lb**; the #1 Pegasus is 30 lb and the #2 Klein is about 26 lb. Pay this off at #1: "two and a half Sidekicks". Also, the Sidekick links to the Pegasus at the same 32 in height, so #6 becomes the #1's extension.
- **C2:** #3 Keter's penalty is a 21.65 in deep top with no nonslip feet. The #2 Klein fixes the depth with a 39.25 × 23.75 in top (largest of the six) at the same 1,000 lb rating.
- "So why isn't it number one?" at #2: no clamps in the box, and no outlet has tested it.

## 8. FREE UPGRADE (candidate — needs one more source before scripting)

**Lock check before load:** before you clamp or cut, push down on each corner and pry on the clamped board, the way Bob Vila's testers did. A clamp that doesn't lock is the one failure they hit (the WEN). It costs nothing, and it works on any bench.
