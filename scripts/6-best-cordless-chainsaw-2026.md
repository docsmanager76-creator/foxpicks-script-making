## HOOK

Same milling test, same wood, same crew running both — one saw cut forty-three inches per watt-hour of battery, and another cut three. A Concord Carpenter ran that test on five saws in one afternoon, and nobody puts a fourteen-times endurance gap on the box. Consumer Reports has tested forty-five chainsaws for its current ratings. TechGearLab has lab-ranked a dozen more. I read all of it, so you don't have to. Today I'm ranking the six best cordless chainsaws of 2026, from six down to one, busting the speed myth that makes most first-time buyers pick the wrong saw, and naming one chainsaw that reviewers still put on best-of lists in 2026 that I would not spend a single dollar on. All the product links are in the description.

## NUMBER 6

Number six, the EGO Power+ CS1611.

This is the saw for someone who has never owned one, and it is the entry point on this list. Pro Tool Reviews named it their best-for-the-money pick in this year's battery chainsaw update, and their reasoning is worth hearing. Do not read entry-level as underpowered. EGO rates it at forty-cc gas equivalent, which covers storm cleanup, firewood rounds, and anything under about twelve inches thick. The bar is sixteen inches, which is where most first-time buyers should be starting anyway. The chain moves at twenty meters per second, or sixty-six feet per second.

And here is the number I want you to hold on to. It weighs nine pounds bare. Nine. Hold that number, because the saw that two separate outlets crowned the best in this entire category weighs exactly double.

Now, the honest limits. Pro Tool Reviews' own note is that it is not as powerful as the higher-priced options, and that is true. Put it into a twenty-inch oak trunk and it will bog down. There is no top-handle version, and sixteen inches of bar caps what you can safely fell.

It is the most affordable pick on this list, it runs the same fifty-six-volt battery as every other EGO tool you will ever buy, and EGO covers the tool for five years and the battery for three. Buy this for yard cleanup and firewood rounds. Do not buy it if you are dropping standing timber.

## NUMBER 5

Number five, the Ryobi 40V HP Brushless twenty-inch.

Now, the myth I promised. Everybody shops chainsaws by cutting speed, because that is the number every review leads with. Watch what it fails to tell you. A Concord Carpenter ran two tests on the same five saws — a speed cut through pine poles, and a milling test measuring inches cut per watt-hour. The saw TechGearLab clocked as the fastest ever tested, the EGO CS2005, managed three inches per watt-hour. Three. The DeWalt that finished dead last on speed managed forty-three. So stop shopping by the stopwatch alone. You are buying a day of work, not one cut.

This Ryobi is why that myth matters, because it is the only saw in that test good at both. Second fastest on the speed cut at sixteen-point-six seconds, second best on endurance at thirty-five inches per watt-hour, eight full cuts through an oak beam, and the lightest of all five at fourteen-point-nine pounds assembled. Concord gave it their Best Value award.

Now, the honest limits. Pro Tool Reviews calls it not as feature-rich, and that is fair. It only exists inside Ryobi's forty-volt world, and you cannot get it shipped from Amazon.

It is the value pick here, and Ryobi covers 40V tools for five years, plus two more years if you register within thirty days. Buy it if you want one saw that does everything adequately. Skip it if you need a specialist.

## NUMBER 4

Number four, the Echo eFORCE DCS-2500T.

This one is not for most of you, and that is exactly why it is on the list. It is a twelve-inch top-handle saw, built to be run one-handed, from a rope, up a tree. Echo made it for working arborists, and Pro Tool Reviews calls Echo's fifty-six-volt top handles some of the best in the emerging professional-grade battery category.

Here is what a top handle actually changes. The handle sits on top of the saw instead of behind it, so the tool balances in one hand while your other hand holds the tree. There is a patented Quick Draw harness ring that lets you hook and unhook the saw from your climbing harness in one movement — forty feet up on a rope, that is a safety feature, not a convenience. The brushless motor uses what Echo calls MaxOut, which holds power output flat as the battery drains instead of fading through the last quarter of the charge.

Now, the honest limits. Twelve inches of bar is twelve inches of bar — this is a limbing and topping saw, not a felling saw. Top-handle saws are genuinely more dangerous in untrained hands, and this one ships with a two-point-five amp-hour battery, which is small for a working day. Echo also warranties the battery for only two years, against five on the tool.

It sits in the professional tier, and Echo covers the tool five years in consumer use and two years in commercial use. Buy this if you climb for a living. If you cut standing on the ground, buy almost anything else on this list.

## VILLAIN BLOCK (not one of the picks)

Before the top three, the promise I made. The DeWalt 60V FLEXVOLT.

Power Tool Insider still calls it a no-brainer if you already run DeWalt, and it sits on almost every best-of list this year. On paper it makes sense — one FLEXVOLT pack runs your twenty-volt tools and this saw.

Then somebody timed it. In A Concord Carpenter's five-saw head-to-head it finished last overall, and last on the speed cut at twenty-three-point-two seconds against the winner's fifteen-point-six. That is forty-nine percent slower on identical poles. They flagged cramped controls and less refined balance.

A saw that shares your battery but wastes half your afternoon is not saving you money, it is spending your time. Buy the DeWalt for the batteries, and put a better saw on the end of them.

## NUMBER 3

Number three, the EGO Power+ CS2005.

This is the fastest cordless chainsaw anybody has put a stopwatch on. TechGearLab made it their Editors' Choice with an overall score of eighty-one, and clocked it through a four-by-four in three-point-four-five seconds — a second and a half faster than the next saw of any kind, and roughly twice as fast as the next electric. Pro Tool Reviews put it against a Stihl and a Milwaukee on an eight-inch oak log and it won there too, at fifteen-point-three seconds against seventeen-point-nine and eighteen-point-nine.

So why isn't the fastest saw here number one? Because of the number from the top of this video. In A Concord Carpenter's milling test, this saw returned three inches per watt-hour. The DeWalt returned forty-three. On an oak beam it managed six full cuts, second to last of five. Concord ranked it fourth overall and wrote that it lacked cutting power and struggled with balance. TechGearLab measured thirty-five minutes of runtime, nineteen pounds and one ounce on the scale, and ninety-nine decibels.

Read that as a job description, not a failure. If your work is short bursts — bucking rounds, clearing a downed limb, storm cleanup in an hour — nothing here touches it. If your work is a full Saturday, it will be on the charger before you are done.

It is a premium-tier saw on the same fifty-six-volt platform as number six, and EGO covers it five years. Buy it for speed. Do not buy it for stamina.

## NUMBER 2

Number two, the Greenworks Commercial 82V H.O.G.

This is the only saw on this list that two independent outlets separately crowned number one. Pro Tool Reviews named it their best battery chainsaw overall for 2026. A Concord Carpenter scored it first of five in their head-to-head and called it a true gas-saw replacement. When two crews testing different wood on different days land on the same machine, that is as close to a settled answer as this category gets.

The numbers are not subtle. Greenworks rates it at eighty-cc gas equivalent, the highest on this list. The chain moves at twenty-nine-point-nine meters per second, which is ninety-eight feet per second. Bars run from twenty up to twenty-eight inches. In Concord's speed cut it was fastest at fifteen-point-six seconds, and in their cross-cut endurance test it made thirteen full cuts through an oak beam while the next best made eight.

Now the number I asked you to hold. The entry-level EGO at number six weighs nine pounds bare. This one weighs eighteen-point-three pounds bare and twenty-four-point-two pounds once the battery and oil are in it. Exactly double, and you feel every ounce over your head.

So why isn't it number one? Because of what that weight takes out of you, and three other things. Concord measured it at one hundred and three-point-five decibels, the loudest saw in their test by eight. It sits at the very top tier of this list. Greenworks covers it for two years, the shortest warranty here, on the top-tier machine here. And it runs through a commercial dealer channel rather than showing up at your door in two days.

Buy this if cutting is your job. Do not buy this to clear one fallen tree a year.

## NUMBER 1

Number one, the Husqvarna Power Axe 350i.

Here is why the reviewers who disagree about everything else keep landing near this saw. Popular Mechanics, who have been testing chainsaws for decades, named it their best electric pick after testing a dozen machines. TechGearLab scored it eighty, level with EGO's Best Buy winner and one point behind their Editors' Choice.

What earns the top spot is that nothing about it is a trade. TechGearLab measured fourteen pounds six ounces, which is nearly five pounds lighter than the EGO at number three. Fifty-one minutes of runtime against that saw's thirty-five. And eighty-two-point-one decibels — the quietest saw in their entire twelve-model lineup, seventeen decibels below the CS2005 and twenty-one below the Greenworks. Eighteen inches of bar, a 7.5 amp-hour battery in the kit, and a boost mode that drops its cut time from seven-point-zero-seven seconds to five-point-one-three when you actually need it.

The honest limits are real. TechGearLab clocked the charge time at two hours forty-five minutes, nearly three hours, the slowest here by a wide margin — so one battery is not enough if you cut all day. It is a forty-volt platform, so it will not share packs with the fifty-six-volt tools most of this list uses. And in boost mode it still will not out-cut the CS2005.

For a saw that is lighter than almost everything, quieter than everything, runs longer than the fastest saw here, carries a three-year warranty, and ships to your door, nothing else on this list is this easy to live with. That's why it's number one.

## CLOSER

Before you pay for anything, ask one question. Not how fast is it — how much wood does it cut per battery? Cut speed is one second of your afternoon. Endurance is the other three hours. That question is the difference between forty-three inches per watt-hour and three, and no product page shows you that number.
