## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST
1. Horsepower vs real need (1/3 – 1/2 HP covers most homes, per TOH + BestReviews)
2. **Flow at 10 ft of lift**, not the max GPH (max is measured at 0 ft)
3. Shut-off head / max lift
4. Housing: cast iron vs thermoplastic vs aluminum (heat shedding, weight)
5. Switch: vertical float vs tethered vs electronic, and the **minimum basin diameter** it fits
6. Impeller and solids handling (vortex, top suction vs bottom suction, screen)
7. Discharge size (1-1/2 in standard = drop-in replacement)
8. Cord length, quick-disconnect, power-indicator plug
9. Thermal overload protection, oil-filled motor
10. Ownership: warranty years, where assembled, what is in the box (check valve? no pick includes one)

## HOOK — type 1 COLLISION (no pick brands)
Two pumps in this video, one a third of a horsepower and one three quarters, **both print the same flow number on their own pages: 4,600 gallons an hour.** The small one's own spec table then says 63 gallons a minute (= 3,780/h). The big one's bullet says 5,490. Three numbers, two pumps, one is lying by omission: the height. Promises: which number is real; the most powerful pump on a major list you should skip; the 3-yr vs 2-yr warranty disagreement; a free 5-minute bucket test.

## MYTH — max GPH predicts how fast your pit empties
Max GPH is measured at 0 ft of lift (BestReviews: figures "are for horizontal pumping"). Your discharge rises 8–12 ft. Wayne CDU790: 4,600 at 0 ft → 3,060 at 10 ft (−33%). Zoeller M53: 43 gpm → 34 gpm at 10 ft. Planted at #5, paid at #1. Line: **"You're buying gallons at your height, not gallons on the box."** Note: Bob Vila printed the M53 as "more than 1,550 GPM" — a unit slip (GPH); we won't mock it, just use maker numbers.

## FREE UPGRADE — the bucket test + check valve look
Pour a 5-gallon bucket into the pit: the float should lift, the pump should start, empty it and shut off (TOH FAQ; inspectandtest "pour a bucket of water into the pit"; TOH "Test your sump pump at least twice a year"). While it runs, look at the check valve: if water falls back into the pit after shutoff, the pump is pumping the same water twice (TOH/Trethewey per summary — attribute to TOH only; inspectandtest "Replacing the valve usually ends the rapid cycling"). Two Zoellers have an LED plug: glance at it. No pick includes a check valve in the box. Dedicated circuit, no extension cord (TOH).
