##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with any system on this list.

These tips come from maker manuals and Consumer Reports, not a lab test.

First, check your water pressure with a gauge on an outdoor spigot. Most tank systems here want at least forty psi.

Second, flush a new system as the manual says, because the first tanks can run cloudy.

Third, put a leak detector or a drip tray under the sink.

Fourth, change pre-filters on time. They protect the membrane, the part that matters most.

Fifth, sanitize a tank system when the manual tells you to. Consumer Reports notes reverse osmosis needs periodic bleach sanitizing.

Sixth, know your water first. Good Housekeeping warns reverse osmosis can be overkill without a water test.
