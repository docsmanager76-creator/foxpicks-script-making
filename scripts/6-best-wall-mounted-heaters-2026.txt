##HOOK
WIRED put a thermometer behind a wall heater that is sold as a piece of wall art. The glass in front reached two hundred degrees at the center. The wall behind it went past eighty, and the metal bars holding it up hit one hundred twenty. That heater is not on this list. It is the villain, and you will hear exactly why. A wall heater is supposed to do one simple thing. It gets heat off your floor and into the room, without cooking the wall it hangs on. So this list covers plug in units and the kind you wire into the wall. You will also hear why the Eco button on the box saves less than you think, using Tom's Guide's own watt meter. And there is a free mounting rule that works for any heater you buy. All the product links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it is about where you drill, not what you buy.

Mount the heater low, and close to where you actually sit. Warm air rises, so a heater up near the ceiling heats the ceiling first. TechRadar warns against mounting too high, and Broan's own grille aims heat down toward the floor for that reason. Tom's Guide found a ten foot sweet spot on a radiant panel, and heat fades past it. Before you drill, check the mounting holes against your studs. Studs usually sit sixteen inches apart. Bob Vila found one heater's holes thirteen and three quarter inches apart, so one screw needed a drywall anchor.

That check costs nothing, and it decides whether your heater warms you or the ceiling.
