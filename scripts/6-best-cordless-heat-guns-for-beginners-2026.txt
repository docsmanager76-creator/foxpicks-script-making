##HOOK
Bob Vila named one cordless heat gun the best cordless you can buy. TechGearLab bought that same gun, ran it beside the others, and gave it the lowest heating score of every cordless model on the bench. It is fast and tiny, and it has exactly one heat. For a pro shrinking wire connectors, that is fine. For a beginner, one heat is the whole problem, because vinyl, shrink tube, and thin plastic want a gentle setting first. So this countdown is built around control, not the biggest number on the box. You will hear why the highest temperature rating is not the gun that strips the most paint. You will meet that award winner before the top three. And there is a free check, before you heat any old paint, that matters more than any gun. All the product links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the WORKPRO W125372AE 7.2V Cordless Mini Heat Gun.

This is for the beginner with no tool batteries at all, who mostly works on crafts, vinyl, resin, and shrink tubing.

The battery is built in. It is a 4000 milliamp hour cell that charges over USB-C. WORKPRO rates it for up to forty minutes on low and twenty five minutes on high. You get three modes, each with its own colored light. Blue is cool air with no heat, yellow is 572 degrees, and red is 842. That cool mode is useful. It lets you test the airflow on a project before any heat touches it. The maker says it reaches 572 in about fifteen seconds, and 842 in about a minute. The whole gun weighs ten ounces. Hold that number, because the best cordless gun on this list weighs more than that with no battery at all. In the box you get a metal stand, a USB-C cable, a narrow spoon nozzle, and fifty heat shrink tubes in mixed sizes.

Now, the honest limits. No outlet has tested it, so these are the maker's numbers. Owners describe the heat as gentle, and WORKPRO itself says it is not for soldering. It will not strip paint or thaw a frozen pipe.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick.

Buy it for crafts at a desk, and skip it for any job on the house.

##P5
Number five, the Craftsman CMCE530B V20 Cordless Heat Gun.

This is for the beginner who already owns Craftsman V20 batteries and wants a heat gun for paint, decals, and shrink tube.

Craftsman rates it at 950 degrees, and it reaches working temperature in seven seconds. Two nozzles come in the box. The flat spreader fans heat across a wide area, for drying, shaping, and stripping paint. The curved deflector wraps hot air around a round part, like shrink tubing on a wire. The body is compact, so it fits inside a cabinet or a wheel well.

Now for the myth I promised. The biggest temperature on the box does not decide how much work gets done. Testix ran a cordless paint stripping test. One gun, rated at over a thousand degrees, cleared about ten square feet in fifteen minutes. Another gun, rated lower at 990, cleared about thirteen square feet in under twelve minutes. The lower number did more work, faster. You are buying control, not the biggest number on the box.

Now, the honest limits. Craftsman rates it at up to fifteen minutes per charge on a four amp hour pack. That is the shortest runtime of the big brand guns here. One owner review also found the casing gets hot in long sessions.

Its place in the lineup is the value pick.

Buy it for short jobs, and skip it for a whole door of old paint.

##P4
Number four, the LIVOWALNY 20V LCD Cordless Heat Gun for DeWalt Batteries.

This is for the beginner who owns DeWalt batteries and wants to see the exact temperature on a screen.

Be clear on one thing first. This is not a DeWalt tool. It is a separate brand built to fit DeWalt 20 volt packs, and no outlet has tested it. A small LCD shows the temperature and the airflow while you work. Mode one runs from 122 to 572 degrees, for vinyl, shrink tube, and crafts. Mode two runs from 572 to 1,022, for paint and adhesive. There are six airflow speeds, and the maker claims up to 9.9 cubic feet per minute. Overheat protection shuts it off if it runs too hot. Five nozzles come in the box: two stepped nozzles for small wires, a reflector for tubing, and a flat nozzle for paint. The listing carries a two year warranty.

Now, the honest limits. It is a 350 watt tool, and it is hungry. The maker's own listing says a two amp hour pack gives you about six minutes. It tells you to use four amp hours or more, and to pull the battery off when you finish, or the gun drains the pack.

Its place in the lineup is the specialist pick, for digital control.

Buy it if you want a number on a screen, and skip it if you only own small batteries.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the promise I made. The Milwaukee 2688-20 M18 Compact Heat Gun.

On paper, it earns its fans. Bob Vila named it the best cordless heat gun. It reaches 875 degrees in about six seconds, and Bob Vila says one charge handles over forty wire connections. Pro Tool Reviews calls it a niche tool that thrives on heat shrink. For an electrician, it is a great tool.

Then TechGearLab put it beside the other cordless guns. It got the lowest heating score of the group. It has one fixed heat, no low setting, no lock for hands-free work, and no nozzles in the box. Testers also found the safety switch sits strangely high.

A heat gun with one setting is a blowtorch with extra steps.

For a first gun, look at number one. Buy the Milwaukee only for wire connectors, or do not buy it.

##P3
Number three, the Ryobi P3150 ONE+ 18V Cordless Heat Gun.

This is for the beginner who owns Ryobi ONE+ batteries, or wants the battery system with the most tools around it.

Ryobi rates it at 875 degrees, and says it reaches working temperature in under ten seconds. ToolBoxBuzz checked that and found it took a little longer, which they did not mind. Pro Tool Reviews named it the best Ryobi heat gun. Two nozzles come in the box. The deflector bends air around shrink tube and pipe, and the concentrator narrows the heat onto a small spot, like a stubborn sticker. Ryobi lists six cubic feet per minute of airflow and an LED work light. It is made for shrink tubing, paint removal, and thawing. Ryobi backs it with a three year limited warranty.

Now, the honest limits. It has one heat setting. Yes, that is the same weakness as the gun I just warned you about. The difference is two nozzles in the box, and a battery system many beginners already own. You control the heat with distance and movement. And Ryobi rates the runtime at up to twenty minutes on its large six amp hour pack. Remember those two numbers, one heat and twenty minutes, because number two fixes both.

Its place in the lineup is the platform pick.

Buy it if you already own Ryobi, and skip it if you need fine control.

##P2
Number two, the Makita XGH02ZK 18V LXT Variable Temperature Cordless Heat Gun.

This is for the beginner who wants every control from day one, and plans to grow into vinyl wrap and paint.

Pro Tool Reviews named it the best cordless heat gun, and called it the first battery model to pass a thousand degrees. Makita rates it at up to 1,022. The key word is variable. You set the temperature to the job, instead of living with one heat. Airflow has two settings, 4.2 and 7.1 cubic feet per minute. The trigger has a lock-on for hands-free work and a lock-off for safety. Remember number three, with one heat and twenty minutes? On the same size six amp hour pack, Makita rates this one at thirty minutes on low and twenty one on high. Makita lists vehicle wraps, window tint, stripping paint and varnish, thawing pipes, loosening tile, and bending plastic pipe. It carries a three year limited warranty.

So why isn't it number one? TechGearLab and Testix both picked a different gun as the best cordless. Testix tested the European version of this Makita and scored it below that pick. And even on a big pack, high heat lasts twenty one minutes.

Its place in the lineup is the premium pick.

Buy it if you want full control, and skip it if you do not already own Makita batteries.

##P1
Number one, the DeWalt DCE530B 20V MAX Cordless Heat Gun.

This is for most beginners, who want one gun gentle enough for vinyl and hot enough for paint.

TechGearLab named it their Editors' Choice for best cordless, and wrote that it rivals top corded models. Testix ranked its European version best overall. It has two settings. Testers list the low at about 550 degrees, and DeWalt rates the high at 990, with 6.7 cubic feet per minute of air. Remember the myth? This was the gun with the lower temperature rating in that paint test, and it stripped more paint, faster. A lock-on button lets you work hands-free, the only one in TechGearLab's test. A lock-off trigger keeps it from firing by accident. An LED lights up dark cabinets. The nozzle is the standard size of corded heat guns, so other attachments fit. A hook nozzle and a flat nozzle come in the box. DeWalt rates it at up to forty two minutes per charge.

Now, the honest limits. Testix got seventeen minutes on high from a five amp hour pack. And remember number six, at ten ounces? This gun weighs more than that with no battery.

Its place in the lineup is the best for most pick, backed by DeWalt's three year limited warranty.

Buy it as your first heat gun, and skip it if you live in another battery system.

That's why it's number one.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it matters more than any gun here.

The enemy is old paint. If your home was built before 1978, the paint on your trim may contain lead. Heat it hard, and that lead can reach the air you breathe. The EPA's lead renovation rule only allows a heat gun on painted surfaces below 1,100 degrees. Every gun on this list tops out under that line, but under the line is not the same as safe. So before you strip anything old, use a lead test swab from the paint aisle. Start on the lowest setting, keep the nozzle moving, and never park it on one spot.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether old trim gets stripped or gets into your lungs.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: what is the lowest heat this gun can do? Every box brags about the top number. The low end decides whether your first vinyl or shrink tube job goes well, and most boxes bury it.

All the product links are in the description. Most of these are tool only, so check whether you need a battery and charger. The LIVOWALNY is not made by DeWalt, so read its battery list before you buy. The villain Milwaukee is linked too, for wire work only.

Quick recap. Crafts at a desk with no batteries: the WORKPRO mini. Short jobs on Craftsman batteries: the CMCE530B. A digital screen on DeWalt batteries: the LIVOWALNY. A Ryobi household: the P3150. Every control on Makita packs: the XGH02ZK. And one first heat gun for most people: the DeWalt DCE530B.
