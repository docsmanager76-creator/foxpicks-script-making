##HOOK
TechRadar's Mark Pickavance reviewed a thermal camera sold on a five-twelve by three-eighty-four number, then looked at the sensor: two-fifty-six by one-ninety-two. He wrote that the picture is interpolated, and is not that sensor. Three out of every four pixels on that screen were never measured by anything.

Not one dishonest brand, either. Every camera in this category does it, under its own invented name: TISR, X-cubed, Super Resolution, UIRA.

So I ranked six of them by the detector, not the sticker. One wins the biggest independent test here on a ninety-six by ninety-six chip. One famous badge fails on a number printed in its own listing. And a strip of electrical tape fixes the biggest error you can make with any of them. All the product links are in the description.

##FREE
One more thing, and it works on every camera here.

Shiny metal lies to you. Bare copper, polished aluminium, a chrome fitting: low emissivity surfaces behave like mirrors in infrared, so the camera reads whatever is reflected instead of the part. That is how people condemn a healthy busbar, or miss a failing one.

FLIR and Fluke both publish the same fix. Press electrical tape on the surface, set emissivity to nought point nine five, and read the tape. Most tape sits at nought point nine five, and FLIR states this puts you back inside about two degrees.

Every pick here has adjustable emissivity. That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether your reading is a diagnosis or a coin flip.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question. What is the native detector resolution, with no enhancement word attached? Not TISR, not Super Resolution, not X-cubed.
