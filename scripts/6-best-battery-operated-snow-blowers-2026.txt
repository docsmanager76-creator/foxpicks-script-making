##HOOK
Cordless snow blowers promise to clear your driveway on a single charge. Then Bob Vila's testers put twelve of them to work for two weeks, on two seventy-five-foot driveways, in temperatures down to minus seven degrees. Typical runtime came out at thirty to forty-five minutes. Throw distance split the field too. The small machines launched snow about twenty feet, and the biggest reached around fifty.

That gap decides whether you clear your driveway in one pass or three, and the number on the box rarely tells you which side you will land on.

Six battery snow blowers made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the VOLTASK 48V Cordless Snow Blower SB-40A-AZ 20-Inch Brushless.

This is for someone with a short driveway, a front walk and a few steps, who wants to stop shoveling without buying a big machine.

The listing gives a twenty-inch clearing path, a brushless motor, and a forty-eight volt system built from two twenty-four volt batteries, with a dual quick charger in the box. It lists a throw of about twenty-five feet and a maximum snow depth of ten inches. Weight is twenty-two point nine pounds, the lightest on this list, so you can lift it over a step. The chute turns with a directional plate, and two LED lights let you clear before sunrise or after dark. The listing says about thirty minutes of runtime, which sits at the bottom of the range Bob Vila measured in the hook.

I could not find a hands-on test of this model from an outlet I trust, so every figure here is the maker's claim.

Now, the honest limits. Without a test, that thirty minutes is a claim, and cold weather cuts battery runtime. It is also a light machine, so deep or wet snow will slow it down.

Its place in the lineup is the entry pick.

Buy it if you clear a short driveway or a walkway. Do not buy it if your driveway holds more than two cars.

##VILLAIN
Before the top two, the one I promised. The RYOBI 40V 22-Inch Two-Stage Cordless Snow Blower.

Here is the steel-man. Bob Vila's team, after testing twelve models, named it the top pick. It runs on two eight amp-hour batteries, throws snow past forty feet, and clears a wide path. It is a two-stage machine with a steel auger.

Then the downsides. It weighs about one hundred thirty-six pounds, and Bob Vila found the batteries drain fast at full speed. That is the thirty-to-forty-five-minute runtime from the hook, and a machine this heavy eats it. At that weight, you also have to push and steer it through every turn.

I found only that one outlet backing the Ryobi, so treat this as one tester's view, not a consensus. For a driveway you clear before work, buy number one or number two.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every machine here.

These tips come from battery maker guidance, not from a lab test.

First, charge and store the batteries indoors. Cold weather cuts lithium-ion capacity, and the guidance expects roughly twenty to thirty percent less runtime near fifteen degrees.

Second, install them just before you start. The first few minutes of use warm them a little, which helps.

Third, keep a spare charged battery for bigger jobs. EGO suggests this for large driveways, and it means a long clear does not stop halfway.

Fourth, clear early and often. Light snow takes less power, and that stretches the thirty to forty-five minutes from the hook. Fifth, do not leave a battery on the charger for months. Take it off once it is full.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this number come from a test or from a label? Thirty minutes, sixty feet and thirty-two cars are claims. About twenty feet of throw, thirty to forty-five minutes of typical runtime and one hundred thirty-six pounds came from testers who measured them.

All seven links are in the description, including the Ryobi. Check the weight, check whether the batteries are in the box, check the warranty, and check how deep your snow gets before you buy.

Quick recap. The VOLTASK is the entry pick. The SENIX is the value pick. The EGO SNT2410 is the specialist. The SNT2807 is the heavy-duty pick. The SNT2405 is the premium pick. And for most people, the EGO SNT2112.

Prices move fast, so check before you buy. One thing: if you own one of these, tell me how the battery held up in your coldest week. If enough of you post, we will compare.
