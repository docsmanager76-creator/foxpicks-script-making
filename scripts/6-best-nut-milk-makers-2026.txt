##HOOK
WIRED named one of these machines its best pick for families and large batches. Its own listing says it makes ten or twenty ounces at a time. That is about two and a half cups. In the same guide, the almond milk pick makes five cups. Engadget timed that one at about ninety seconds of blending. Reviewed timed the family pick at twelve to sixteen minutes for twenty ounces. So the label on a list does not always match the jug. This list sorts six machines by what they actually pour. You will hear why the words no straining needed do not promise smooth milk. You will hear about a machine testers loved that quietly got hard to buy. And there is a free habit that improves the milk from any machine. All the product links are in the description.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works in every machine on this list.

The enemy is not the motor. It is the ingredient and the clock. Reviewed made almond milk twice, once from dry almonds and once soaked, and the soaked batch came out creamier and less bitter. So soak almonds overnight, even in a machine that says no soak. For coconut, Homes and Gardens found dry flakes alone made watery milk, and soaking them with dates and cashews fixed it. Then make smaller batches. Engadget notes homemade milk stays fresh only three to five days in the fridge. A batch you pour out is a batch you paid for twice. Keep the pulp for baking.

That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether your machine replaces the carton or the carton wins.
