##HOOK
One inch hole. Same concrete. Forty-five seconds with one SDS-plus rotary hammer, seventy with another, and over two minutes with a third. Bob Vila's team ran seven drills through four tests in concrete, brick and cinder block, and the gaps were that wide. They timed every hole.

Impact energy did not explain it. The hammer rated at one point seven foot-pounds took over two minutes. The one rated at one point nine took forty-five seconds.

Anchor holes are smaller, but the half-inch times lined up in the same order. Six SDS-plus hammers made this list, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

A safety clutch is built in to protect your wrist if the bit binds. The maker lists a twenty-four month warranty, and the kit comes with three SDS-plus bits and a flat chisel.

Buy it if you want a corded SDS-plus hammer for occasional anchor work. Do not buy it if you need a test result behind the claims, or if you will drill all day.

##FREE
Here is the free upgrade, and it works with every hammer here.

Hole quality decides how the anchor sets. Simpson Strong-Tie's own engineering blog, written by Derek Gilbert in April twenty twenty-five, lists the steps for its Titen HD concrete screw.

First, use a carbide bit the same diameter as the anchor. Second, drill deeper than the anchor needs, because that extra depth gives dust a place to settle. Third, blow the hole clean with oil-free compressed air. The blog says overhead holes may skip that step.

Fourth, use the depth rod or depth stop on your hammer, so every hole stops where it should.

None of that costs anything, and it matters more than the energy rating.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does this number come from a test or from the maker? Three joules, five point five foot-pounds, fifty-three hundred blows per minute: those are claims. Forty-five seconds, seventy, fifty, and over two minutes came from a team that drilled the holes.

All seven links are in the description, including the Makita. Check the weight, check the warranty, and check that you own the battery before you buy.
