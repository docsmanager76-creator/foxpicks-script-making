##VILLAIN

Spruce Eats names it Most Versatile, and Food & Wine's guide walks you through making yogurt in one. On paper it is clever. One pot boils the milk and then incubates it. Spruce measured it bringing milk to exactly 180 degrees.

Consumer Reports tested the smaller three-quart Duo in 2021. At the default eight hours, the yogurt still looked like milk. At fifteen hours it was chunky in places and runny in others, and it never turned tangy.

A yogurt button that can't hold a steady temperature is a pressure cooker with extra steps.

Buy it for pressure cooking and treat the yogurt button as a bonus. For yogurt, buy number one, or don't buy it.

##FREE
Here's the free upgrade, and it works with every machine here. These machines only hold milk warm. You do the rest. Serious Eats, Food & Wine and Spruce Eats all say to heat the milk to 180 degrees first. Serious Eats holds it there at least ten minutes. Then let it cool to about 110 before the starter goes in. Add it hot and you kill the cultures. Add it cold and the batch crawls. No thermometer? Serious Eats offers a finger test. You should be able to hold a finger in the milk for three to five seconds. That habit costs nothing, and it decides whether you make yogurt or warm milk.
