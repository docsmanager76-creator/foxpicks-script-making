##HOOK
Four hand mixers, four wattage claims. Two hundred twenty watts. Three hundred fifty. Four hundred. Five hundred. If wattage decided everything, the five hundred would walk away with this list, and it doesn't. The mixer Reviewed named best overall in its hands-on test is the two hundred twenty watt one.

Reviewed's tester, Sharon Franke, has reviewed kitchen equipment for over thirty years, and what she noticed was not watts. It was how the mixer starts, how it holds, and whether it can knead a real loaf of dough.

Six hand mixers made this list, every one with nine speeds, and I'll tell you which is number one and why. Before that, one villain. All the links are in the description.

##P6
Number six, the CBQ 9-Speed 400W Digital Hand Mixer.

This is for someone who wants a modern digital mixer with nine speeds and does not plan to knead bread every week.

The listing gives a four hundred watt DC motor, nine speeds with a soft start that raises the speed gradually, a digital display and touch buttons. It comes with two flat beaters, two dough hooks and one whisk, all three hundred four stainless steel and listed as dishwasher safe. A snap-on storage case is included, and the maker lists a twelve-month warranty. The maker also claims it runs as quiet as sixty decibels.

Remember that four hundred watt number, because one more mixer on this list claims the same.

Now, the honest limits. I found no independent test of this mixer, so every number above is the maker's own description. The quiet claim is a claim, not a measurement. The warranty is also the shortest of the ones the listings state.

Its place in the lineup is the entry digital pick.

Buy it if you want a digital display and a storage case at the entry point. Do not buy it if you need a test result behind the claims.

##P5
Number five, the Lord Eagle 9-Speed 500W Digital Hand Mixer.

This is for someone with a small kitchen and a sore wrist who wants the lightest machine on this list.

The listing gives a five hundred watt DC motor, nine speeds with a turbo button and a soft start, and a digital screen. It weighs about eight hundred grams, which the listing says is one point seven six pounds. It includes two beaters, two dough hooks, a whisk and a silicone spatula, with a one-button eject. Weight matters because Reviewed found that beating eggs and sugar for a long time tired the arm, even with the lightest models.

Now, the honest limits. I found no independent test of this model, so the five hundred watts and the fifty decibels are claims. It also stores in a bag, not a hard case, so the beaters can slide around in a drawer.

Its place in the lineup is the lightweight pick.

Buy it if weight is your first worry. Do not buy it if you want proof that five hundred watts means more kneading power.

Buy it if you want a longer warranty on a digital mixer. Do not buy it if you want a bread test first.

##VILLAIN
Before the top three, the one I promised. The Breville the Handy Mix Scraper BHM800SIL, nine speeds plus boost.

Reviewed gave it real credit: quiet, easy to care for, dishwasher safe attachments, a light and a timer, and a pause button. The listing says its Beater IQ detects the attachment and adjusts the speed. That is the steel-man.

Then Reviewed's own test. It called the Breville one of the slower models and the only one that struggled to knead enough dough for two loaves. It said the egg whites weren't particularly airy, the speed controls were confusing, and cost was a con. At more than three pounds, it was hard to hold for more than a minute or so.

One outlet, praise and trouble in the same review. I could not read a second outlet, so I won't name one.

Buy number one or number three instead. Skip this one.

##P3
Number three, the Braun MultiMix 5 HM5100 9-Speed Hand Mixer.

This is for someone who bakes for a long stretch and wants the weight out of their hand.

Reviewed's tester said the MultiMix wowed her with speed. It mixed cake batter quicker than most, and its beaters gave the highest volume when whipping egg whites. The motor sits up front, not under the handle, so it is easy to hold during long jobs. The speed dial is set before you turn it on, and when you stop to scrape, you don't have to reset it. It is the only one without air vents, so there are no slots to pick batter out of, and the beaters are dishwasher safe. Reviewed lists two pounds.

The listing gives nine speeds plus turbo and a three hundred fifty watt motor.

Now, the honest limits. Reviewed says that motor position makes it tall, so it moves less fluidly through thick dough. It does not come with a whisk, only two dough hooks and two multi-whisk beaters.

Its place in the lineup is the easy-to-hold specialist.

Buy it if long sessions tire your arm. Do not buy it if thick dough is your main job.

##P1
Number one, the Cuisinart Power Advantage Plus HM-90 9-Speed Hand Mixer.

It mixed evenly without stopping to scrape, and the on and off switch and speed controls were intuitive. It always starts slowly, so you never get a puff of flour in your face. Reviewed also found the most helpful manual of any mixer it tested, with recipes you might actually make.

The listing gives nine speeds, a two hundred twenty watt motor, and a SmoothStart feature with three low speeds that cut splatter. The extra-long beaters have no center posts and are dishwasher safe. That's why it's number one.

##FREE
Here is the free habit, and it works with every mixer here.

First, start low. Reviewed noted that the Cuisinart always starts slowly, so there is no flour in your face. Every mixer on this list has a soft start or low speeds, so use them. Add dry ingredients at speed one or two, then raise it.

Second, set the dial before you turn it on. Reviewed says the Braun's speed dial is set before it starts, and you don't have to reset it after you pause to scrape.

Third, switch hands. Reviewed said beating eggs and sugar for a long time was fatiguing, even with the lightest models, and the testers often switched hands.

All three cost nothing, and they matter more than any wattage number.

##TAIL
Before you pay, ask one question: does the number on the page come from a tester or from the maker? Four hundred watts, five hundred watts, sixty decibels, fifty decibels: those are claims. Kneading dough as well as a human hand, and always starting slowly, came from a tester who did the work.

All seven links are in the description, including the Breville. Check the warranty, check the weight, and buy from a seller you trust.
