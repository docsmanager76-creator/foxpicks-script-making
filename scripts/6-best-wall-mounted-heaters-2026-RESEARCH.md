## STEP 0 — BUYER DECISION CHECKLIST
1. Install type: plug-in over an outlet vs hardwired/recessed (electrician, 120 V or 240 V)
2. Heat type: infrared/radiant vs PTC fan vs fan-forced coil vs mica panel
3. Room-size claim vs measured warm-up
4. Measured noise vs claimed dB
5. Power draw per mode (does Eco actually draw less)
6. Safety: tip/overheat cutoff, cool-touch grille, IP rating, ALCI plug, child lock, UL/ETL
7. Controls: thermostat range, timer, app/voice, remote
8. Mounting: template, stud spacing, cord length, weight
9. Ownership: warranty, washable filter, what is in the box
10. Placement: height, distance from where you sit

## HOOK — 5 Single Absurd Number (recommended)
"One of these six on Eco mode drew one thousand three hundred ninety-six watts. On full blast, one thousand three hundred ninety-eight." Tom's Guide, watt meter. Fair nuance (must be said): Eco then dropped to idle sooner, which is where any saving comes from. No pick brands in the hook; Tom's Guide named. Pays off at #5.
Alternative: 6 Category Lie on room-size claims (one Heat Storm listing: 150 sq ft primary; Bob Vila's list printed up to 1,000; This Old House 750).

## MYTH — "the room-size number tells you how warm you'll be"
- Heat Storm HS-1500-PHX: listing says 150 primary / 750 supplemental, Bob Vila's wall list printed 1,000, This Old House printed 750. Measured: +5°F in 18 minutes (Bob Vila) and +5°F in a 150 sq ft room (Tom's Guide).
- Boldr Kelvin: felt within a 10-ft sweet spot (Tom's Guide).
- Rule: compare measured degrees per minute, and read "primary" sq ft only. Plant at #6, pay at #1.
- Bonus material: the 28 dB claim vs Tom's Guide's 39-45 dBA (room floor 37.0) vs Bob Vila's ~28 dB in Eco. Speak it as a disagreement, never resolved silently.

## VILLAIN — C Overpriced Premium: Boldr Kelvin
- Steel-man: Tom's Guide calls it a premium wall-mounted alternative and "nearly silent"; Reviewed praises its looks and efficiency; IP44, 2-yr warranty.
- Kill shot (WIRED, measured): it takes about an hour to heat the glass, the glass center reaches 200°F, the wall behind tops 80°F, and the mounting bars reach 120°F. Tom's Guide's own test: a 10-ft sweet spot, beyond which it only slowly raises the room temperature. 450 W standard model, rated 100 sq ft.
- Not sold on Amazon (Scrape.do search found none). V row links shopboldr.com, noted as the villain, not a pick, not rule-checked.
- Placement: after #4, before the top three.

## FREE UPGRADE — mount it low and close to where you sit (new, not a repeat of last video)
- TechRadar: don't mount it too high, because hot air rises.
- Tom's Guide (Kelvin): radiant heat works inside about 10 ft, so mount it near the couch or desk.
- Bob Vila: studs sit 16 in apart and the Heat Storm holes sit 13.75 in apart, so expect one drywall anchor. Check the hole spacing before you drill.
- Cadet listing: install hardwired units on an interior wall.
- Add-on (Tom's Guide method): check the breaker first. The reviewer confirmed a 15 A breaker; the listings show 12.5 A draw.
